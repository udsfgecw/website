### HARD WATER

Water described as "hard" is high in dissolved minerals, specifically calcium and magnesium. Hard water is not a health risk, but a nuisance because of mineral buildup on fixtures, poor soap and detergent performance and also causes formation of scales in boilers.

### TYPES OF HARDNESS

Hardness are of two types temporary and permanent hardness

**Difference between temporary and permanent hardness of water**

| Temporary Hardness | Permanent Hardness |
| --- | --- |

| Can be removing by boiling. | Can't be removing by boiling. |
| Hardness removal is cheap. | Hardness removal is expensive |

### UNITS OF HARDNESS

There are five units , commonly used in hardness measurements

**1. Parts per million (ppm):** It is the number of parts of $\text{CaCO}_3$ equivalent hardness present in $10^6$ parts of water. ie, $1\text{ ppm} = 1\text{ part of }\text{CaCO}_3\text{ equivalent hardness} / 10^6\text{ parts of water}$

**2. Milligram per litre (mg/L):** It is the number of milligrams of $\text{CaCO}_3$ equivalent hardness present in one litre of water.ie $1\text{ mg / L} = 1\text{ mg of }\text{CaCO}_3\text{ equivalent hardness} / 1\text{ L of water}$

But, $1\text{L} = 1000\text{g} = 10^6\text{ mg}$ Hence $1\text{ mg / L} = 1\text{ mg of }\text{CaCO}_3\text{ equivalent hardness} / 10^6\text{ mg of water} = 1\text{ ppm}$

4. Degree French ($^{\circ}\text{Fr}$): It is the number of parts $\text{CaCO}_3$ equivalent hardness in $10^5$ parts of water. ie, $1^{\circ}\text{ Fr} = 1$ part of $\text{CaCO}_3$ equivalent hardness / $10^5$ parts of water

The Hardness of water is expressed as weight in grams of $\text{CaCO}_3$ in one million grams of water (ppm-parts per million) or it is the number of milligrams of $\text{CaCO}_3$ present in one liter water. Calcium carbonate is chosen as the standard for expressing hardness due to the following reasons:

1. $\text{CaCO}_3$ is found to be the most insoluble salt that can be precipitated during water treatment.

2. A standard solution of hard water can be prepared by weighing fixed amount of $\text{CaCO}_3$, dissolving in dil. $\text{HCl}$ and making up to the known volume.

3. The molecular weight of $\text{CaCO}_3$ is 100 (equivalent weight $= 50$), so the calculation is easy

*Disadvantages of hard water*

* It doesn't form lather with soap solution. Instead it produces sticky precipitates of Ca-Soap and Mg-Soap. This will leads to the wastage of soap.
* Hard water used in boilers cause problems such as boiler corrosion, scales and sludges inboilers, priming and foaming, caustic embrittlement, etc.
* The boiling point of hard water is high. So it leads to the wastage of fuel.
* Tea and coffee prepared in hard water have an unpleasant taste.
* It cannot be used in paper industry, because it will affect the smoothness, glossiness,colour, etc. of the paper.

### Ion Exchange process

* Ion exchange resins are insoluble cross linked long chain organic polymers having micro porous structure, where the functional group attached to the chain are responsible for their ion exchange capacity.
* Cation exchange resin (RH+) contains acidic functional groups like –COOH, -SO3H, etc.
* Anion exchange resin (ROH-) contains basic functional groups like –OH, -NH2,

*Cation exchange resins-* are basically styrene-divinyl benzene copolymers, which on sulphonation or carboxylation attain the capability to exchange its H+ ions with cations of water.

*Anion exchange resins* -are basically styrene-divinyl benzene or amine formaldehyde Co polymers containing quartarnary ammonium group, which on treatment with NaOH attain the capability to exchange its OH ions with cations of water.

![Chemical structure of Cation exchange resin](/assets/notes/me/s1/chemistry/module-4/cation-exchange-resin.svg)

![Chemical structure of Anion exchange resin](/assets/notes/me/s1/chemistry/module-4/anion-exchange-resin.svg)

### Procedure:

Initially hard water is allowed to pass through cation exchange resin column to remove all the cations like Ca2+, Mg2+, etc.

$$2\text{R}^-\text{H}^+ + \text{Ca}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{R}_2\text{Ca} + 2\text{H}^+$$

$$2\text{R}^-\text{H}^+ + \text{Mg}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{R}_2\text{Mg} + 2\text{H}^+$$

Then the hard water is allowed to pass through anion exchange resin column to remove all the anions like $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$, $\text{Cl}^-$

$$2\text{R}^+\text{OH}^- + \text{Cl}^- \rightarrow 2\text{RCl} + 2\text{OH}^-$$

$$2\text{R}^+\text{OH}^- + \text{SO}_4^{2-} \rightarrow 2\text{RCl} + 2\text{OH}^-$$

The H+ ions and OH- ions produced from cation and anion exchange resin column combine to form water.

<!-- page 5 | Page 5 -->

$$\text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

When the exchanger becomes exhausted, i.e. its capacity to exchange $\text{H}^+$ ions and $\text{OH}^-$ ions is lost, it is regenerated. Cation exchangers are regenerated by passing $\text{dil.HCl}$ or $\text{dil. H}_2\text{SO}_4$ through the column.

$$\text{R}_2\text{Ca} + 2\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 2\text{RH}^+ + \text{Ca}^{2+}$$

Anion exchangers are regenerated by passing $\text{dil.NaOH}$ through the column.

$$\text{R}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{OH}^- \rightarrow 2\text{R}^+\text{OH}^- + \text{SO}^{2-}$$

**Advantages:**

1. Even highly acidic alkaline water can be softened by this method.
2. Gives water of very low hardness ($2\text{ ppm}$).
3. Water obtained by this method can be used even in high pressure boilers because of its low hardness.
4. No sludge is formed.

**Disadvantages:**

1. Equipment and chemicals used are costly.
2. Turbid water cannot be treated by this method.

**DE-SALINATION BRACKISH WATER**

Water containing high concentration of dissolved salt with a brackish taste is called brackish water. Eg: Brine Brackish water is unfit for drinking. Hence it needs desalination. Reverse osmosis is the best method used for the desalination of brackish water.

***Reverse osmosis***

* When two solutions of unequal concentrations are separated by a semipermeable membrane, then the solvent flows from dilute to the concentrated solution due to osmosis.
* But if the hydrostatic pressure is greater than the osmotic pressure, then the solvent flows through the semipermeable membrane in a direction opposite to that of natural osmosis i.e. from concentrated to the dilute solution. This is called reverse osmosis.
* In this process , pressure of the order $15$ to $40\text{ kg/cm}^2$ is applied to sea water to force its pure water out through the semi-permeable membrane , leaving behind the dissolved dissolves.
* Membranes made of polymethacrylate , polysulphones etc.

*Disadvantages*

* High pressure has to be maintained throughout the process which is very difficult.

**SEWAGE (Waste water)**

Sewage is the liquid waste which includes human waste, house hold waste, industrial waste, etc. It contains 99.99% of water and 0.01% of organic matter, inorganic matter, suspended impurity, colloidal impurity, etc. Sewage contains both aerobic and anaerobic bacteria that bring out the oxidation of organic compounds in it. ### DISSOLVED OXYGEN

- Dissolved oxygen refers to the free, uncombined oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) that is present in water.
- The oxygen that is part of the water molecule ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) is chemically bonded and does not count as dissolved oxygen.
- **Importance:**
  DO is used to measure water quality and is essential for the survival of fish and other aquatic organisms.
- **Factors Affecting Dissolved Oxygen (DO):**

1. **Temperature:**
Cold water holds more DO.
Warm water holds less DO.

2. **Altitude / Atmospheric Pressure:**
Higher altitudes have lower air pressure, so less oxygen dissolves in water.

3. **Salinity (Salt Level):**
Higher salt content reduces dissolved oxygen.
Freshwater holds more DO than seawater.

4. **Temperature Discharge (Thermal Pollution):**
Warm water released from industries or power plants reduces DO levels.

5. **Organic Waste / Pollution:**
Organic matter (sewage, food waste, plant decay) is **broken down by bacteria**.
This process **consumes oxygen**, reducing DO.

6. **Aquatic Plants and Photosynthesis:**
During **daytime**, plants produce oxygen $\rightarrow$ **DO increases**.
At **night**, plants respire $\rightarrow$ **DO decreases**.

**BIOLOGICAL OXYGEN DEMAND (BOD)**

- **Definition:**
  BOD is the amount of oxygen needed by aerobic microorganisms to break down biodegradable organic matter present in 1 litre of water over 5 days at $20^{\circ}\text{C}$.
- High BOD means the water has a lot of waste. Microorganisms use more oxygen to break down this waste. The dissolved oxygen in water becomes low. Low oxygen makes it hard for fish and other water animals to survive.
- **Significance:**
- Used as an indicator of water pollution level in lakes, rivers, and ponds.
- Helps determine water quality and safety for aquatic organisms.

**Chemical Oxygen demand (COD)**

**Definition**

- COD is defined as the amount of oxygen required for the complete oxidation of biologically active and biologically inert materials present in one litre sewage water using chemical oxidising agents such as $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$
- Consequently COD values are greater than BOD values
- The measurement of COD takes only 3 hours compared to that of BOD which takes five days. COD values can be employed to measure approximate value of BOD.

| BOD | COD |
| --- | --- |
| BOD measures the oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose biodegradable organic matter. | COD measures the oxygen required to chemically oxidize both biodegradable and non-biodegradable matter. |
| Test takes 5 days to complete. | Test takes about 3 hours. |
| BOD value is always lower. | COD value is always higher. |

* Trickling filter is a biological treatment method used to treat sewage.
* The filter is rectangular and about 2 meters deep.
* It is filled with crushed stones, broken bricks or similar materials.
* Microorganisms grow on the surface of these stones/bricks.
* A rotating distributor spreads sewage over the filter bed.
* The sewage trickles down through the filter material.
* As it moves down, microorganisms feed on and break down the organic matter.
* The treated (clearer) water is collected at the bottom through the drainage system.
* This process reduces organic impurities and improves water quality.

## Climate change

Climate change denotes the long-term changes in typical temperature and weather patterns of earth. The prominent changes include rise in sea level, changes in precipitation, wind speed, drought, extreme weather events etc. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), climate change is considered the greatest threat to human health in 2021. The composition of atmosphere can change

both naturally and anthropogenically. The key reason for climate change is the emission of anthropogenic (man-made) greenhouse gases.

### Greenhouse gases and Greenhouse effect

#### Greenhouse Gases

* Greenhouse gases trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere.
* Major greenhouse gases are Carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$), Methane ($\text{CH}_4$), Nitrous oxide ($\text{N}_2\text{O}$), Ozone ($\text{O}_3$), Water vapour ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).
* These gases increase due to burning fossil fuels, deforestation, agriculture, industries, vehicles, etc.

#### Greenhouse Effect

* The greenhouse effect is the warming of the Earth's surface due to greenhouse gases trapping heat.
* Sunlight enters the atmosphere, warms the Earth, and heat tries to escape.
* Greenhouse gases absorb and reflect this heat back, keeping the Earth warm.
* A natural level of greenhouse effect is necessary for life.
* But excess greenhouse gases cause global warming and climate change.

### Ozone Depletion

* Ozone layer is present in the stratosphere (upper atmosphere).
* It protects Earth by absorbing harmful UV rays from the sun.
* Ozone ($\text{O}_3$) is formed when oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) is broken by sunlight and recombines:
* $\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{O} + \text{O}$
* $\text{O}_2 + \text{O} \rightarrow \text{O}_3$
* The amount of ozone stays balanced normally because it is being formed and broken at the same rate.

#### Cause of Ozone Depletion

* The main reason for ozone layer damage is Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).
* CFCs are used in:

Refrigerators
Air conditioners
Aerosol sprays

## Solid Waste Management

Solid waste is the unwanted or useless solid materials generated from combined residential, industrial and commercial activities in a given area.

It may be categorized based on its
- Origin- domestic, industrial, commercial, construction or institutional.
- Contents- organic material, glass, metal, plastic paper etc.
- Hazard potential- toxic, non-toxin, flammable, radioactive, infectious etc.

### Composting

Composting is an aerobic biological process in which microorganisms like bacteria and fungi decompose organic waste such as food scraps and plant materials into a humus-like substance. During decomposition, heat is released and the temperature rises, allowing thermophilic bacteria to

<!-- page 20 | Vermicomposting -->

continue the process. After a few weeks, the waste gets stabilized into nutrient-rich compost. The final compost is brown, earthy-smelling, and is used as a natural fertilizer for plants.

### Vermicomposting

* Vermicomposting is a method of composting that uses earthworms, mainly red worms, to decompose organic waste.
* Waste like food scraps is placed at the top layer, and worms move upward to feed on it.
* As they eat the waste, they produce worm castings, which form nutrient-rich compost at the bottom.
* Care must be taken not to add too much food, because high heat can kill the worms.
* Under good conditions, red worms can convert half of their body weight of waste into compost each day.

### Advantages of composting

* It recycles the nutrients and returns them to the soil as nutrients.
* Clean, sand cheap method.
* Reduces pathogens.
* It helps to keep soil cool in summer and warm in winter.
* This can be used as an organic fertilizer.

### Land fills

* Landfills are places where waste is buried in the ground.
* A pit is dug, garbage is dumped, and then it is covered with soil to prevent flies and rats.
* Every day, waste is pressed and covered, forming layers (cells).
* When the landfill is full, it is covered with mud and the land may later be used as a park or parking area.

### Problems with Landfills

* When rainwater passes through waste, it becomes dirty and pollutes soil and groundwater.
* This polluted liquid is called leachate (leaching).
* Landfills also produce bad smell and methane gas, which can cause explosions or contribute to global warming.

### Advantages of landfill

* No air pollution from burning.

- No fire hazards.
- Infectious diseases can be minimized (since the rats and other pests cannot breed in covered pits).

### Sanitary Landfills

- Sanitary landfills are improved landfills designed to reduce pollution.
- They have liners (plastic or clay) at the bottom to stop waste from leaking into soil or water.
- Drainage pipes are installed to collect and treat leachate before releasing it.
- They are more hygienic but very costly to build.

### Problems with Sanitary Landfills

- Plastic liners can crack when they react with chemicals.
- Very little oxygen in tightly packed waste slows down decomposition.
- Biodegradable waste may not decompose for many years.
- They produce methane gas, but in some places, methane is collected and used as fuel.

### Incineration

Incineration is a thermal waste treatment method in which solid waste is burnt in large furnaces at high temperatures. It is used both on small and large scales.

Points:

- In incineration, waste is burnt and converted into ash, flue gases, and heat.
- It is commonly used to dispose hazardous and medical waste, as the high temperature destroys pathogens and harmful organisms.
- It reduces the volume of waste significantly, thus requiring less disposal space compared to landfills.
- The heat produced during incineration can be used to generate electricity or steam, helping in energy recovery.
- However, the process releases toxic gases and fly ash, which may contain dioxins and heavy metals, leading to air pollution.
- The remaining ash, when disposed in landfills, may contaminate soil and groundwater due to leaching, making environmental management necessary.
- **So incineration is used only as a last option due to pollution concerns**
