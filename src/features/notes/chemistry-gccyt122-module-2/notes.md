The Daniel cell consists of two electrodes of dissimilar metals, Zn and Cu; each electrode is in contact with a solution of its own ion; Zinc sulphate and copper sulphate respectively.

* At the anode (negative electrode), zinc metal has greater tendency for oxidation than copper, zinc atom leaves its electrons to the zinc electrode and goes to the solution as $\text{Zn}^{2+}\text{ions}$. Hence Zinc electrode acquires a negative charge.

$$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{e}^-$$ 

* At the cathode (positive electrode), copper is reduced as per the following reaction:

$$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{e}^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)}$$

Positively charged copper ions in the solution side move towards the positive electrode and get deposited.

<u>*The total reaction is:*</u>

$$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)}.$$

* In a Daniell cell electrons flow from zinc electrode to copper electrode through an external circuit, while metal ions form one half cell to the other through the salt bridge.
* Here current flows from copper electrode to zinc electrode that is cathode to anode via an external circuit.
* Daniell cell is a reversible cell while a voltaic cell may be reversible or irreversible.

<u>*Cell representation*</u>

$$\text{Zn}/\text{Zn}^{2+} \; || \; \text{Cu}^{2+}/\text{Cu}$$

<!-- page 4 | Functions of Salt bridge -->

To maintains electrical neutrality in two compartments by allowing movement of anions towards anodic compartment and cations towards cathodic compartment salt bridge is used.

### Functions of Salt bridge

* A salt bridge acts as an electrical contact between the two half cells.
* It prevents mechanical flow of solution, but it provides a free path for the migration of ions, to maintain an electric current through the electrolyte solution. It prevents the accumulation of charges.
* A salt bridge helps in maintaining the charge balance in the two half cells.
* A salt bridge minimizes or eliminates the liquid junction potential.

### ❖ <u>Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)</u>

It consists of inert platinum electrode dipped in $1\text{ M HCl}$. Which is in contact with $\text{H}_2$ gas at $1\text{ atm}$ pressure at $298\text{ K}$

### ➢ <u>Characteristics of SHE</u>

### 1. To know the relative ease of oxidation and reduction:

Greater the reduction potential, more easily the substance undergoes reduction and is a better oxidising agent. (Eg. Fluorine). Similarly greater the oxidation potential, more easily the substance undergoes oxidation and is a better reducing agent. (Eg.Pottasium).

### 2. To predict whether the metal will react with acid to give hydrogen gas:

Metals present above hydrogen in the electrochemical series can react with acids to give hydrogen gas. This is because these metals have greater tendency to undergo oxidation with respect to hydrogen, since they have negative reduction potential.

$\text{Zn} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{H}_2$

### 3. To calculate the standard emf of the cell:

$E^\circ \text{Cell} = E^\circ \text{Cathode} - E^\circ \text{Anode}$

If we know the position of electrodes in the electrochemical series, we can easily predict which electrode can function as anode and which one can function as cathode.

A metal with lower reduction potential can displace a metal with higher reduction potential from its salt solution. $\text{Zn}$ can displace $\text{Cu}$ from $\text{CuSO}_4$ solution.

$\text{Zn} + \text{CuSO}_4 \rightarrow \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{Cu}$

### Glass electrode

- Glass electrode is an example for ion selective electrode.
- It is a special type of glass with low melting point and high electrical conductivity.
- It consists of a thin glass bulb in which $\text{Ag}$ wire coated $\text{AgCl}$ is used as an internal reference electrode.
- It is then filled with $0.1\text{N}$ $\text{HCl}$ solution. $\text{HCl}$ in the bulb furnishes a constant $\text{H}^{+}$ ion concentration.
- The electrode representation is written as

$\text{Ag/AgCl}/0.1\text{M } \text{H}^{+}$.

Finally an equilibrium is established between ($\text{Na}^+$) ions of glass & $\text{H}^+$ ions of solution.

$E_g = E_g^0 - 0.0591 \log 1 / [\text{H}^+]$

### Determination of PH using glass electrode

Glass electrode is used as an internal reference electrode. In order to determine the unknown PH of a solution, glass electrode is coupled with SCE and the resultant cell can be represented $\text{Hg}/\text{Hg}_2\text{Cl}_2/\text{KCl}//\text{H}^+(\text{unknown})/\text{glass}/\text{H}^+(0.1\text{M})$

$E_{\text{cell}} = E_{\text{SCE}} - E_{\text{glass}}$

$E_{\text{cell}} = 0.2422 - (E_g^0 - 0.0591 \text{ pH})$

$E_{\text{cell}} = 0.2422 - E_g^0 + 0.0591 \text{ pH}$

The equation has the form of $y = mx + c$. The equation for a straight line having intercept $c$ and slope $m$ when we plot emf on $y$-axis and pH on X-axis. The constant $c$ and $m$ can be determined by calibrating the pH metre with two buffer solution of known pH.

Intercept $c : 0.2422 - E_g^0$

Slope : $0.0591$, $X = \text{pH}$

### Advantages of glass electrode

2. It can be use to find the pH of oxidising, reducing and even coloured solutions.

Cell constant: In a conductivity cell, the distance between the two electrodes (l) and the area of electrodes are fixed. This constant is called cell constant . unit of cell constant is cm -1 or m-1

### Conductivity cell

A conductivity cell consists of two electrode plates coated with finely divided platinum black (to minimize polarization effects). These two are welded with the ends of platinum wires that are sealed through two glass tubes. The tubes are strongly fixed such that distance between electrodes remains unaltered.

### Determination of Cell Constant

Cell constant may be obtained by measuring 'l' and 'a'. But it is very difficult to measure the actual area precisely in this type of small cells. In actual practice cell constant is measure by filling the conductivity cell with a solution of known conductivity like 0.1 M KCl solution. Conductivity (specific conductance) of 0.1 M KCl solution is 0.01288 S cm-1 at 298 K. By measuring conductance of 0.1 M KCl solution in the given cell, the cell constant can be determined.

**Cell constant= conductivity of 0.1 M KCl solution(k) / conductance (C) of 0.1 M KCl solution**

### Measurement of conductivity using digital conductivity meter

Conductivity cell is connected to the digital conductivity meter and appropriate temperature is selected. Conductivity cell is then dipped into the sample solution taken in a beaker. Make sure that the cell is fully immersed in the solution. Measure the conductance of the solution by selecting the proper range switch (milli S or micro S).

**<u>H$_2$ - O$_2$ FUEL CELL ( Hydrogen -Oxygen Fuel Cell)</u>**

* A fuel cell is a galvanic cell in which the chemical energy of a fuel is directly converted to electrical energy.
* It is an electrochemical conversion device ie; it produces electricity by converting the chemical energy of a fuel and an oxidant, which reacts in the presence of an electrolyte and not by combustion.
* It differs from ordinary electrochemical cell in the sense that, here the reactants are supplied from outside rather than forming an integral part of the cell and the energy should be withdrawn indefinitely from a fuel cell as long as the outside supply of the fuel is maintained.

Hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell produces electricity based on the following exothermic reaction

$$\text{2H}_{2(\text{g})} + \text{O}_{2(\text{g})} \rightarrow \text{2H}_2\text{O}_{(\text{l})}$$

Here hydrogen acts as the fuel and oxygen acts as the oxidant, hence its simply known a hydrogen fuel cell.

* It consists of two electrodes made up of porous graphite, impregnated with metal catalysts such as platinum.
* Arrhenius acid like phosphoric acid is generally employed as the electrolyte A fabric like matrix made of teflon bonded silicon carbide dispersed with highly concentrated phosphoric acid (H$_3$PO$_4$) acts as the electrolyte. This electrolyte matrix transports the hydrogen ions
* . Hydrogen and oxygen gas under pressure (50 atm) are continuously passed through the anodic and cathodic chambers respectively. The cell operates at about a temperature of 150- 200 $^\circ$C since the ionic conductivity of phosphoric acid is very low at low temperatures.
* Heat is generated during the reaction of hydrogen with oxygen, and so cooling plates are used to maintain a cell operating temperature of about 200 $^\circ$C

$2\text{H}_{2(g)} \rightarrow 4\text{H}^+_{(aq)} + 4\text{e}^-; \text{E}^0 = 0\text{V}$

**At Cathode:** Oxygen getting reduced to water at the cathode by consuming the $\text{H}^+$ ions from the electrolyte. $\text{H}^+$ ions from the electrolyte are transported to the oxygen compartment and combines with oxygen gas and electrons to form water. $\text{H}^+$ ion deficiency at the electrolyte side is balanced by the $\text{H}^+$ ion formed at the anode. ### CORROSION

Corrosion is the slow process of deterioration or destruction of the metal through the chemical or electrochemical changes taking place at its surface. Corrosion reactions are of two types. On the basis of this, theories of corrosion are of two types.

I. Dry corrosion (Chemical corrosion)

II. Wet corrosion (Electrochemical corrosion)

#### Dry corrosion:

Corrosion of this type gases occurs by the direct chemical attack of atmospheric gases on the metal in the absence of moisture. They are of three types.

1. Oxidation corrosion

2. Corrosion by other gases

3. Liquid metal corrosion

#### Wet corrosion (Electrochemical corrosion)

This type of corrosion can occur when a conducting liquid is in contact with a metal or when two dissimilar metals or alloys are partially immersed in a conducting solution.

❖ **<u>Galvanic series</u>**

Relative corrosion affinities of metals and alloys can be explained by using a series called galvanic series. This series can be prepared by studying the corrosion affinities of metals and alloys in unpolluted sea water without their oxide films. ## CORROSION CONTROL METHODS

### Cathodic Protection:
In this method, the metal to be protected is made as cathode so that corrosion will not occur.

The following methods are employed for cathodic protection.

#### *a) Sacrificial anodic protection:*
In this method, the metal to be protected is connected by a wire to a more anodic or active metal. Then the more active metal will undergo corrosion and the cathode remains protected. Here the more active anodic metal sacrifices for the protection of cathodic metal and is called sacrificial anode. Metals commonly used for this purpose re Mg, Zn, Al and their alloy

A wire connects the Mg block to a large horizontal cylindrical structure representing a protected pipe. Electrons (e⁻) flow along the wire from the anode to the pipe. The text below the diagram reads 'Sacrificial anodic protection'.



#### *b) Impressed current cathode protection:*
In this method an impressed current is applied in opposite direction to nullify the corrosion current. As a result corroding metal is converted from anode to cathode. The impressed current required can be obtained from a battery with an insoluble anode made of graphite or high silica iron. This method is used for the protection of open water box coolers, water tanks etc.

### ❖ Electroless plating (Autocatalytic plating)

It is the method of depositing a metal from its salt solution on the catalytically active surface of the metal to be plated using a suitable reducing agent without using electrical energy. In this process, metal piece to be plated is immersed in a mixture of reducing agent and a complex compound. The reducing agent reduces metal ion into metal which gets plated over the catalytically active surface of the metal to produce a thin uniform coating. This process is a controlled chemical reduction and is catalyzed by the metal or alloy being deposited. This method allows the easy preparation of films of low thickness using simple equipments. Most common electroless plating is electroless nickel plating.

### ➢ Electroless Copper plating

In this method, article to be plated is immersed in a plating bath containing $\text{CuSO}_4$ (As a source of Cu), formaldehyde (Reducing Agent), buffer solution of $\text{NaOH}$ and a complexing reagent. Air is bubbled slowly through the medium to control the formation of cuprous oxide. Electroless plating of Cu takes place as follows.

#### <u>*Oxidation*</u>

$$2\text{HCHO} + 4\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{ (Gas)} + 2\text{HCOO}^- + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2\text{e}^-$$

#### <u>*Reduction*</u>

$$\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2\text{e}^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}$$

#### <u>*Net reaction*</u>

<!-- page 24 | Applications of electroless Cu plating -->

$$2\text{HCHO} + 4\text{OH}^- + \text{Cu}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{H}_2 \text{ (Gas)} + 2\text{HCOO}^- + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Cu}$$

### _Applications of electroless Cu plating_

* Widely used for metalizing printed circuit boards.
* Used for plating on non-conductors.
* It is also used for making decorative plating on plastics.

### _Advantages_

* This method produces even coatings.
* It does not use electrical power.
* This coating is having improved hardness, strength, ductility and resistance to corrosion.

### _Disadvantages_

* The reducing agent, formaldehyde is a human health hazard.
* The instability of the electroless copper bath creates difficulties in process control.

## ELECTROPLATING

Electroplating is the most important and the most common industrial method for producing metallic coatings. This is the process by which the coating metal is deposited on the base metal by passing a direct current through an electrolytic solution, containing the soluble salt of the coating metal. The film so produced is quite uniform.

**Theory of electroplating:** The object to be plated (base metal) is made as the cathode of the electrolytic cell whereas the anode is either the coating metal itself or an inert material of good electrical conductivity like graphite. The electrolyte is a soluble salt solution of the coating metal. When DC current is passed through the electrolyte electrolysis occurs and the metal is coated on the surface of the substrate.

## Objectives of electroplating

To increase the resistance to corrosion

To increase the resistance to chemical attack and wear resistance

To improve the physical appearance and hardness

To improve the surface properties

To increase the decorative and commercial values of metal

## COPPER ELECTROPLATING

The article to be electroplated is first treated with organic solvent to remove oil, grease etc. Then it is made free from surface scales, oxides etc. by treating with dil. $\text{HCl}$ or dil. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.

The cleansed article is then made as the cathode of the electrolytic cell. Pure copper metal rod is taken as the anode. Both the electrodes are dipped in copper sulphate solution (electrolyte) taken in an electroplating tank. Direct current is passed through the electrolyte, electrolysis occurs and $\text{Cu}$ ions move towards the cathode and gets deposited there

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Cathode} & \quad \text{Cu}^{2+} + 2\text{e}^- \rightarrow \text{Cu} \\
\text{Anode} & \quad \text{Cu} \rightarrow \text{Cu}^{2+} + 2\text{e}^-
\end{aligned}$$

The free $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ ions migrate to the copper anode and dissolve an equivalent amount of copper.

If the anode is made of some inert material like graphite, then electrolytic salt is added continuously in order to maintain proper coating metal ion concentration in the electrolytic bath. Moreover, the process of electroplating goes on non-stop, since the anode is not consumed and its replacement is not required. When inert electrode is used, anode reaction can be represented

$$\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{H}^+ + 1/2\text{O}_2 + 2\text{e}^-$$

## Applications of copper electroplating

* Steel articles used in automobile industry are generally given three successive coatings of Cu, Ni and Cr. This is done for corrosion protection and to impart shining and clear metal appearance.
* Copper electroplating is used in decorative items, kitchen wares, jewelry etc. to enhance the aesthetic appearance
