## ENGINEERING MATERIALS

### FUELS

A fuel can be defined as any combustible substance which during combustion gives large amount of industrially and domestically useful heat. During the process of combustion of a fuel, the atoms of carbon and hydrogen combine with oxygen to form $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ with the simultaneous liberation of heat at a rapid rate.

$$\text{Fuel} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{Products} + \text{Heat}$$

The products have less energy when compared to the reactants. The difference in energy is emitted in the form of heat.

### CALORIFIC VALUE

Calorific value of a fuel is "the total quantity of heat liberated from the combustion of a unit mass or unit volume of the fuel in air or oxygen". Calorie is the unit of heat, which is defined as the amount of heat required to increase the temperature of one gram of water by one degree centigrade.

#### Higher or Gross Calorific Value (HCV)

Gross or Higher calorific value is the amount of heat liberated, when unit mass or unit volume of the fuel has been burnt completely and the product of combustion are cooled to room temperature.

***NOTE : Usually, all the fuels have substantial amount of hydrogen. This hydrogen during combustion is converted into steam. As the products of combustion are cooled down to room temperature, the steam gets condensed into water and thus the latent heat of condensation of steam also gets included in the measured heat, which is then called higher or gross calorific value.***

<!-- page 3 | Low or Net Calorific Value (LCV) -->

## Low or Net Calorific Value (LCV)

Low or Net calorific value is the amount of heat liberated, when unit mass or unit volume of the fuel has been burnt completely and the product of combustion are allowed to escape.

**LCV = HCV - Latent heat of water vapour formed**

1 part by mass of hydrogen gives 9 parts by mass of water according to the equation

$$H_2+O_2 \rightarrow H_2O$$

$$LCV=HCV-0.09H \times 587\text{cal/g.}$$

Where H is the % of hydrogen in the fuel and 587 cal/g is the latent heat of steam.

## DETERMINATION OF CALORIFIC VALUE OF SOLID FUEL USING BOMB CALORIMETER

The calorific value of solids and non-volatile liquids is determined by Bomb calorimeter

**Construction: Bomb calorimeter consists of the following parts,**

* A strong cylindrical stainless steel bomb in which the combustion of fuel is made to take place.
* The bomb is provided with a gas tight screw cap or lid and the lid is provided with two stainless steel electrodes and an oxygen inlet valve.
* To one of the electrodes, a small ring is attached which can act as a support for a nickel or stainless steel crucible.
* The bomb is placed in a copper calorimeter containing a known amount of water and the calorimeter is provided with an electrical stirrer and a Beckmann thermometer (which can read accurately temperature difference up to 1/100th of a degree).
* The copper calorimeter is surrounded by an air jacket and water jacket to prevent loss of heat due to radiation.

**Working:** A known mass of the given fuel (about 0.5 to 1 g) is taken in a clean crucible supported over the rings .A fine Mg wire is stretched across the electrodes touching the fuel sample. The lid of the bomb is tightly screwed and bomb is filled with oxygen at 25-30 atmospheric pressure. The bomb is then lowered into copper calorimeter, containing a known mass of water. The initial temperature of water is noted after thorough stirring. The electrodes are then connected to 6V battery and the circuit is completed. The sample burns and heat liberated is transferred to water. Uniform stirring of water is continued and the maximum temperature attained is recorded. The calorific value of the fuel is calculated as below

**Calculations**

$$\text{Mass of fuel sample taken} = Xg$$
$$\text{Mass of water in calorimeter} = Wg$$
$$\text{Water equivalent of calorimeter, stirrer, thermometer, bomb etc.} = w$$

**Note: w = Wt. of apparatus $\times$ Sp. Heat of the metal**

$$\text{Initial temperature of water} = T_1$$
$$\text{Final temperature of water} = T_2$$
$$\text{Gross or Higher calorific value} = L$$
$$\text{Heat liberated by burning of the fuel} = XL$$
$$\text{Heat absorbed by water and apparatus'} = (W + w) \times \text{specific heat of water} \times (T_2 - T_1)$$
$$= (W + w)(T_2 - T_1)$$

**Note: Sp. heat of water = 1 cal/g/$^\circ$C**

$$\text{But, heat liberated by fuel} = \text{Heat absorbed by water, apparatus etc.}$$
$$\text{i.e., } XL = (W + w)(T_2 - T_1)$$
$$\text{HCV or } L = \frac{(W + w)(T_2 - T_1)}{X}\text{ cal/g}$$

**Net or Low Calorific Value (LCV)**

$$\text{Let percentage of hydrogen in fuel} = H$$
$$\text{Therefore, the grams of hydrogen present in } 1\text{ g of fuel} = 1 \times H/100$$

Hydrogen is converted to steam according to the equation,
$$H_2 + \frac{1}{2}O_2 \longrightarrow H_2O$$
$$2\text{ g} \qquad\qquad 18\text{ g}$$
$$1\text{ g} \qquad\qquad 9\text{ g}$$

$$\text{i.e., Wt. of water produced from } 1\text{ g of } H_2 = 9\text{ g}$$
$$\text{Wt. of water produced from } H/100\text{ g of } H_2\text{ or } 1\text{g of fuel} = 9 \times H/100\text{g} = 0.09Hg$$
$$\text{Latent heat of steam} = 587cal/g$$
$$\text{Latent heat of water vapour} = 0.09H \times 587cal$$
$$\text{LCV or Net calorific value} = HCV - 0.09H \times 587\text{ cal/g}$$

**Corrections:** To get more accurate results the following corrections must be considered.

***Fuse wire correction:*** The heat liberated, as measured above, includes the heat given out by ignition of the fuse wire used. Hence it must be subtracted from the total value.

**Acid correction:** During combustion, sulphur and nitrogen present in the fuel are oxidized to their corresponding acids under temperature and pressure of ignition.

$$\text{S} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{SO}_2$$

$$\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$$

**<u>Gross calorific value</u>**

**Cooling correction:** If the time taken for the water in calorimeter to reach the maximum temperature from room temperature is 'x' minutes and the rate of cooling is dT/dt, then the cooling correction is given by x.dT/dt. This should be added to the observed rise in the temperature

**Gross calorific value=** (W+w) (T2-T1 + cooling correction) - (acid + fuse correction)/mass of the fuel .

One part of hydrogen combines with 8 parts of oxygen to form water, i.e. for every 8 part of oxygen; one part of hydrogen is fixed. Therefore,

Amount of hydrogen available for combustion = Total mass of hydrogen — 1/8 mass of oxygen in fuel

### Analysis of coal

The composition of coal varies according to the source and age. In order to ascertain the quality of coal, it is subjected to different types of analysis such as proximate and ultimate analysis.

### Proximate Analysis

The proximate analysis gives information regarding the practical utility of coal. This analysis is called so because the data collected vary with the procedure adopted for the analysis like temperature of heating, duration of heating, the material of the crucible used etc.

This analysis involves the following determinations

1. **Moisture:** A known amount of finely powdered coal sample is heated in a silica or porcelain crucible at $105-110^{\circ}\text{C}$ for about $1\text{ hour}$ in an electric oven. The crucible is then cooled in a desiccator and weighed. The loss in weight is reported tas moisture on a percentage basis.
$$\%\text{ moisture} = \frac{\text{Loss in weight} \times 100}{\text{Weight of coal taken}}$$

2. **Volatile matter:** The volatile matter in coal mainly consists of combustible gases like $\text{H}_2$, $\text{CO}$, $\text{CH}_4$ and other hydrocarbons. The moisture free coal left in the crucible after the previously mentioned process is then covered with a lid and heated in a muffle furnace, maintained at $\sim 950^{\circ}\text{C}$ under inert atmosphere for $7\text{ minutes}$. The crucible is then cooled first in air, then in desiccator and weighed. The loss in weight is reported as volatile matter.
$$\%\text{ volatile matter} = \frac{\text{Loss in weight due to removal of volatile matter} \times 100}{\text{Weight of coal taken}}$$

3. **Ash:** It is a non-combustible matter which is left behind when all the combustible sub- stances have been burnt off from coal. Ash content is determined by heating the residual coal left after the removal of volatile matter in an open crucible at $700-750^{\circ}\text{C}$ for half an hour in a muffle furnace. Heating, cooling and weighing is repeated till a constant weight is obtained.
$$\%\text{ ash} = \frac{\text{Weight of ash formed} \times 100}{\text{Weight of coal taken}}$$

4. **Fixed carbon:** The material left behind after the determination of moisture, volatile matter and ash is known as fixed carbon.
$$\%\text{ of fixed carbon} = 100 - \%\text{ of (Moisture} + \text{Volatile matter} + \text{Ash)}$$

### Significance of proximate analysis

<!-- page 8 | KNOCKING -->

Proximate analysis provides following valuable information in assessing the quality of coal.

- **Moisture Content:**

Water in coal lowers its energy because heat is wasted to evaporate moisture. So, coal with less moisture gives more useful energy and is better as fuel.

- **Volatile Matter:**

Coal with a lot of volatile matter burns with a long, smoky flame and loses some fuel as gas or vapor. This reduces energy efficiency and requires more space in the furnace. So, less volatile matter means better quality coal.

- **Ash Content:**

Ash is the leftover, non-burning part of coal. It lowers the energy and blocks air and heat flow, reducing furnace efficiency. High ash also damages equipment. Therefore, coal with less ash is better.

- **Fixed Carbon:**

This is the solid part of coal that actually burns and produces heat. More fixed carbon means more energy and better coal quality. It also helps in designing efficient furnaces.

### KNOCKING

- Knocking is a sharp, rattling noise inside the engine.
- It happens when the fuel-air mixture ignites too early or unevenly inside the engine.
- In diesel engines, some types of diesel (with branched or aromatic compounds) cause delayed burning, which leads to knocking.
- In petrol engines, sometimes the fuel-air mix explodes before the spark plug ignites it, causing knocking.
- This knocking disturbs the smooth movement of the piston.
- Knocking damages the engine parts like piston and cylinder.
- It also wastes fuel and reduces the engine's efficiency.

### <u>CETANE NUMBER</u>

- The cetane number shows how easily and quickly diesel fuel catches fire inside a diesel engine.
- When diesel is sprayed into the engine, it needs to ignite quickly for smooth running.
- A higher cetane number means the fuel ignites faster, so the engine works better without knocking or roughness.

<!-- page 9 | OCTANE NUMBER -->

- A lower cetane number means the fuel takes longer to ignite, causing engine noise and less efficiency.
- So, higher cetane number = better quality diesel fuel.

The hydrocarbon cetane (n-hexadecane) has a very short ignition delay and an arbitrary cetane value of 100 has been assigned to it. On the other hand 1-methyl naphthalene has very long ignition delay and hence, O value has been assigned to it.

Cetane number of a diesel fuel can be defined as the percentage of cetane in a mixture of cetane and 1-methyl naphthalene which will have same ignition characteristics as the fuel under examination and under the same set of conditions.

### OCTANE NUMBER

- Octane number tells how well petrol (gasoline) resists knocking in an engine.
- Knocking happens when petrol burns too early or unevenly, causing noise and damage.
- A higher octane number means the petrol can withstand more compression without knocking.
- So, higher octane fuel = smoother engine running and less knocking..

Since n-heptane knocks very badly, its antiknock value is given the arbitrary value '0', whereas 2,2,4-trimethyl pentane has very little knocking and hence is assigned an antiknock value '100'. Thus, the octane no. of a gasoline is defined as the percentage of iso-octane present in a mixture of iso-octane and n-heptane, which matches the fuel under test in knocking characteristics.

Higher the octane no., greater is the anti-knock property of the fuel

### Biodiesel

- Biodiesel refers to a non-petroleum based fuel consisting of short chain alkyl esters.
- It is a diesel like fluid obtained from materials of biological origin like liquids accumulated in plants and algae or from hydrocarbons produced by some plants and algae
- Fatty acid methyl ester of vegetable oil has similar physical and chemical properties like diesel and hence it is called biodiesel.

### Production of biodiesel

Bio-diesel can be prepared from any lipid oils of rapeseed, rubber seed, jatropha or even from animal fat. Production of biodiesel from rapeseed involves the following steps:

i ) Production of rapeseed oil by crushing of rapeseed. The oil produced is heated to $100^{\circ}\text{C}$ to make it free from moisture.

ii ) Heating of rapeseed oil with methanol at $50^{\circ}\text{C}$ in the presence of $\text{NaOH}$ so that glycerol and fatty acid methyl ester are formed.

$$\text{Rapeseed oil} + \text{Methanol} \rightarrow \text{fatty acid methyl ester} + \text{glycerol}$$

iii ) The glycerol is allowed to settle and the fatty acid methyl ester is purified and used as a biofuel.

### Advantages

i. It is $98\%$ biodegradable

ii. It is nontoxic.

iii. The raw material is renewable.

iv. Its contribution to greenhouse effect is less than that of diesel.

v. Energy yield is high.

## GREEN HYDROGEN

The need of renewable/alternate energy is now becoming increasingly urgent to ensure energy security, as there is a rapid depletion of fossil fuel resources. Moreover, the long-dominated use of fossil fuels has resulted in air pollution, climate change and environmental degradation also. Hydrogen energy is emerged as an effective solution for global energy crisis. Approximately $80$ million tons of hydrogen is produced world-wide per annum. Major percentage of this hydrogen is produced from fossil fuels.

<!-- page 11 | Page 11 -->

Based on the production methods, hydrogen is classified into

i. **Blue hydrogen:** Hydrogen is produced through the partial oxidation of methane with O2, and the carbon dioxide formed is then captured and stored.

ii. **Grey hydrogen:** Hydrogen is produced through the partial oxidation of methane with Oxygen, and the carbon dioxide formed is not captured.

iii. **Brown hydrogen:** Produce hydrogen and carbon dioxide using partial oxidation of coal.

iv. **Pink hydrogen:** Hydrogen is produced by the electrolysis of water using nuclear energy.

v. **Green hydrogen:** It refers to the hydrogen gas produced by the electrolysis of water using renewable energy sources. This results in the production of hydrogen without the emission of any green house gases and hence it is otherwise mentioned as sustainable hydrogen.

The core step involved in green hydrogen production is the electrolysis of water. Water splits it into hydrogen and oxygen by passing electric current through it.

$\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow \text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$

A typical water electrolysis cells (known as electrolyzer) is composed of an electrolyte and two electrodes (cathode and anode) which enables the decomposition of water molecules. hydrogen gas is produced at the cathode and oxygen gas at the anode. There are mainly three types of electrolyzers based on electrolytes-separators used.

i Alkaline water electrolyzer (AWE): Uses alkaline electrolytes such as KOH and NaOH.

ii Proton exchange membrane water electrolyzer (PEMWE): Uses solid polymer elec- trolyte membrane such as perfluorosulfonic acid to conduct protons.

iii Solid oxide water electrolyzer (SOWE): Uses solid oxide or ceramic electrolyte.

Solar energy, wind energy, geothermal energy, hydro power etc, can be used as the renewable energy sources for green hydrogen production. Green hydrogen can be produced by electroly- sis water using renewable energy sources like solar electricity or by photolysis of water.

**Applications**

1. Used in fuel cells to produce electricity in a sustainable way.
2. Can be used to produce ammonia in an ecofriendly way (green ammonia).
3. Used for the production of natural gas within power plants while reducing the carbon intensity.

## LUBRICANTS

Any substance introduced between two relatively moving surfaces for reducing friction and wear is called lubricants. The process of reducing frictional resistance between moving surfaces by the application of lubricants is called lubrication.

### Functions of lubricants

1. It reduces the loss of energy in the form of heat.
2. It increases the efficiency of machine.
3. It prevents the interlocking or inter-joints welding at the surface asperities.
4. It protects the materials from corrosion.
5. Sometimes it stops the leakage of gases or pressures from the cylinder.

**Classification of grease: Greases are classified based on the soap used in their manufacture.**

#### a. Lime or Calcium Soap Grease (Cup Grease)

- Made by emulsifying petroleum oil with calcium soap.
- Water resistant (insoluble in water).
- Cannot be used above $65^{\circ}\text{C}$ (oil and soap separate).
- Commonly used in ball bearings.
- Cheap and widely available.

#### b. Lithium Soap Grease

- Made by thickening petroleum oil with lithium soap.
- Water resistant and withstands high temperatures.
- Good mechanical stability and low oxidation tendency.
- Suitable for low temperatures (about $-15^{\circ}\text{C}$).
- Used as multipurpose grease.

#### c. Sodium Soap Grease

- Made by dispersing sodium soap in petroleum oil.
- Water soluble (not water resistant).
- Can be used up to $175^{\circ}\text{C}$.
- Fibrous structure helps in high-temperature applications.
- Suitable where lubricants heat up due to friction.

#### d. Aluminum Soap Grease

- Made by dispersing aluminum soaps in oils.

* Waterproof due to low soap content.
* Not suitable above $90^\circ\text{C}$.
* Limited to moderate temperature applications.

**e. Axle Grease (Resin Grease)**

* Made by reacting slaked lime with resin oil and fatty oils at $58^\circ\text{C}$.
* Fillers like talc and mica are added.
* Water resistant.
* Suitable for heavy equipment use.

### Liquid Lubricants

Liquid lubricants are widely used in internal combustion engines. Apart from reducing friction and wear, they prevent gas leakage between piston and cylinder.

#### Lubricating oils

**a. Vegetable Oils & Animal Oils**

* Natural lubricants.
* Have good oiliness (stick well to metal surfaces).
* Tend to oxidize easily and decompose at high temperatures.
* Form acidic and thick products on exposure to air.
* Usually blended with mineral oils to improve performance.

**b. Mineral Oils**

* Obtained by fractional distillation of petroleum.
* Most widely used lubricants.
* Cheap, abundant, and stable under working conditions.
* Poor oiliness compared to vegetable/animal oils.
* Oiliness improved by adding oleic acid, stearic acid, etc.

**c. Synthetic Oils**

* Man-made lubricants (not derived directly from natural sources).
* Made from chemical compounds like:
* Poly alpha olefins, Polyesters, etc.
* Designed to offer high performance, especially under extreme conditions.

### Properties of Lubricants

Viscosity is the property of a liquid or fluid by virtue of which it offers resistance to its own flow. When a liquid is flowing steadily over a fixed horizontal surface, its layer in immediate

contact with the fixed surface is stationary and velocity of the layers increase with the distance from the fixed surface.

**Viscosity index (V.I.):** The variation of viscosity with respect to temperature is indicated by viscosity index. The viscosity index is a measure of rate at which the viscosity of an oil changes with temperature. For a good lubricating oil, the variation of viscosity with temperature should be small, i.e. viscosity index is high.

**Measurement of viscosity index**

For the measurement of viscosity index, two types of standard oils are used.

1. Paraffin-based Pennsylvanian oils (V.I = 100, designated as 'H' oils).
2. Naphthenic–based Gulf oils (V.I = 0, designated as 'L' oils).

Pennsylvanian oils show a small decrease in viscosity whereas Gulf oils show a large decrease in viscosity with increase of temperature. The viscosity of lubricating oil is measured by Redwood viscometer. The viscosity of the oil under test is measured at $100^{\circ}\text{F}$ and also at $210^{\circ}\text{F}$. Let these values be 'U' and 'V' respectively. The difference between the two values should be low if the oil is good. From the list of 'H' oils, the oil which has the same viscosity at $210^{\circ}\text{F}$ is selected and its viscosity is measured at $100^{\circ}\text{F}$, let it be 'H', then from the list of 'L' oils, the oil which has the same viscosity at $210^{\circ}\text{F}$ is selected and its viscosity is measured at $100^{\circ}\text{F}$, let it be 'L'. The viscosity index of the oil under test is given by

$$V.I = \frac{L - U}{L - H} \times 100$$

**FLASH AND FIRE POINT**

**Flash Point:**

- Lowest temperature at which oil gives off vapors that ignite briefly.
- The flame appears for a moment and then goes out.
- Indicates the starting point of flammability.
- Used to check the safety of storage and handling.

**Fire Point:**

- Lowest temperature at which oil gives off vapors that keep burning.
- The flame continues to burn for at least 5 seconds.
- Indicates when the oil can sustain a fire.
- Used to assess fire risk during operation.

**Cloud and pour point**

Lubricating oils are often made from petroleum.

These oils contain impurities like: Paraffin wax, Asphalt, Resinous substances

At low temperatures, these impurities can separate out from the oil.

*The cloud point is:*

*The temperature at which the oil starts to look cloudy or hazy.*

*The pour point is:*

*The temperature at which the oil stops flowing (becomes too thick to pour).*

Both cloud point and pour point show how stable the oil is in cold conditions.

*The pour point helps determine if the lubricant is suitable for machines operating in low temperatures.*

**ANILINE POINT**

- The Aniline Point of an oil is the lowest temperature at which the oil is completely miscible with aniline (an organic compound) in equal volumes. 2. Importance of Aniline Point
- It indicates the aromatic content of the oil:

Low aniline point → High aromatic content  
High aniline point → High paraffinic (alkane) content

**How is the Aniline Point Determined?**

- **Equal Mixing**

Take equal volumes of the oil and fresh, dry aniline in a clear glass test tube or bottle.

- **Heating**

Heat the mixture gently with stirring until a clear, homogeneous (single-phase) solution is formed.

- **Cooling**

Allow the mixture to cool slowly, while continuing to stir.

- **Observation**

The temperature at which the mixture just becomes cloudy (or separates into two layers) is noted.

That temperature is recorded as the Aniline Point of the oil.

## CEMENT

Cement is a light greenish heavy powder which may be described as "a material possessing adhesive, cohesive properties and capable of binding materials like stones, bricks etc". Cements are hydraulic in nature ie., it possesses the property of setting and hardening under water. The essential constituents of cement are compounds of Calcareous (Ca) and argillaceous (Al+Si) materials.

### Classification of cement

Cements can be classified into the following types:

#### 1. Natural Cement

* Made by heating natural limestone that contains some clay.
* No extra materials are added.
* Sets quickly but has low strength.
* Not commonly used now (replaced by Portland cement).

#### 2. Puzzolana Cement

* One of the oldest types of cement.
* Made by mixing volcanic ash (puzzolana) with lime (no heating needed).
* Has hydraulic properties (can set under water).
* Not used alone today – usually mixed with Portland cement for better performance.

#### 3. Slag Cement

* Made from blast furnace slag (a waste from steel production) and lime.
* Slag is cooled quickly in water, dried, mixed with lime, and ground.
* Sets slowly, has low strength, and is not very durable.
* Used in large concrete works where high strength is not needed.

#### 4. Portland Cement

<!-- page 17 | Manufacture of cement Raw materials for the manufacture of cement are: -->

- Most common and important cement today.
- Made by heating limestone and clay together at high temperature (about 1500°C).
- After cooling, gypsum is added to control setting time.
- Strong, reliable, and versatile – used in almost all construction work.

5. Special Cements

These are modified forms of Portland cement, made for specific purposes:

| Type | Simple Use / Feature |
| :--- | :--- |
| White Cement | For decorative work and tiles (white in color). |
| Rapid Hardening Cement | Sets and gains strength quickly (used in repairs). |
| Low Heat Cement | Produces less heat while setting (good for dams). |
| High Alumina Cement | Very strong and heat resistant (used in furnaces). |
| Coloured Cement | White cement + color pigments (for designs). |
| Sulphate Resisting Cement | Resists chemicals (used in sewage or marine works). |

### Manufacture of cement Raw materials for the manufacture of cement are:

1. Calcareous materials ($\text{CaCO}_3$) such as limestone, chalk, etc.
2. Argillaceous materials ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{SiO}_2$) such as clay, shales, slate etc.
3. Powdered coal or fuel oil
4. Gypsum
5. Iron oxide

### Functions of the ingredients of cement

1. Lime is the principal constituent of cement. Excess of lime and lesser amount of lime reduces the strength of cement.
2. Silica imparts strength to cement.
3. Alumina makes the cement quick setting.
4. Gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4$) helps to retard the setting action of cement.
5. Iron oxide provides colour, strength and hardness to cement.
6. $\text{SO}_3$ in small proportions, imparts soundness to cement.

### The manufacture of Portland cement consists of the following steps.

**<u>Step A: Mixing Raw Materials (Limestone + Clay in 3:1 ratio)</u>**

**1. Dry Process**

**Used when limestone and clay are hard.**

**Steps:**

- Break limestone into small pieces.
- Mix with clay (3:1 ratio).
- Pulverize into fine powder.
- Feed into a rotary kiln.

**2. Wet Process**

**Used when limestone and clay are soft.**

**Steps:**

- Limestone is crushed and powdered.
- Clay is washed with water (removes impurities).
- Mix clay paste and powdered limestone (3:1 ratio).
- Grind into a slurry (contains ~40% water).
- Feed into the rotary kiln.

**Step B: Calcination (Burning in Rotary Kiln)**

Rotary kiln = A long, rotating steel tube lined with fire bricks.

Heated up to 1750°C.

Raw materials stay inside for 2–3 hours.

**Zones Inside the Kiln:**

**Drying Zone (~750°C):**

- Water evaporates from slurry.
- Clay breaks into basic components: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{SiO}_2$, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$.

**Calcination Zone :it is the middle part of the klin rotator(~1000°C):**

Limestone or slurry undergoes decomposition to form lime(calcium oxide ) and carbondioxide

($\text{CaCO}_3$) $\rightarrow$ $\text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$ (Calcium oxide is formed).

**Clinkering Zone:** it is the lower part of the klin rotator here lime and clay undergoes chemical interaction or fusion ,giving calcium aluminates or calcium silicates **(~1600°C):**

<!-- page 19 | Step C: Formation of Clinkers -->

Materials fuse and react to form:

Di-calcium silicate ($\text{C}_2\text{S}$) : $2\text{CaO} + \text{SiO}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CaO}\ \text{SiO}_2$

Di-calcium aluminate ($\text{C}_2\text{A}$) $\rightarrow 2\text{CaO} + 2\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 \rightarrow 2\text{CaO}\ \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$

### Step C: Formation of Clinkers

The calcium silicates and aluminates fuse together to form small hard grey colour stones called clinkers

- Small, hard, grey balls called clinkers are formed
- They are very hot (~1000°C).
- Clinkers are cooled and collected.

### Chemistry of Clinkering

- Clay contains silica and aluminosilicate.
- During heating, $\text{CaO}$ breaks the covalent bonds in silica networks.
- New ionic compounds form (cement compounds).

### Step D: Mixing with Gypsum

- Clinkers are ground into fine powder in a ball mill.
- 2–3% gypsum is added.
- Gypsum slows down setting (acts as a retarder).
- Final product: Portland Cement.
- Cement is then packed automatically.

### Setting and Hardening of Cement

$\text{Cement} + \text{Water} \rightarrow \text{Hydration Reaction.}$

- This leads to:
- Stiffening (initial thickening).
- Setting (becomes rigid).
- Hardening (gains strength).

### Microstructure Formation:

## NANOMATERIALS

Nanomaterials are materials whose characteristic length scale lies within nanometric range ( 1-100nm ) at least in one dimension, they are objects with any one of the three external dimensions at the nanoscale. The behaviour of materials may depend more on surfacr area than particle composition itself.

### CLASSIFICATION

- **_Classification based on dimension :_** This is the classification based on the number of dimensions , which are not confined to the nano scale ( less than 100nm)

<!-- page 25 | Classification based in materials -->

- **Zero dimension( 0 -D) :** Here all the three dimensions are in the nanometric range eg. Nano particles

- **One dimension ( 1 -D) :** Here one of the dimensions is outside the nanometric range and the other two are within the range eg. Nanowires, fibres

- **Two dimension ( 2 -D ) :** Here two of the dimensions are outside the nanometric range and one is within the range eg Nanofilms , layers etc

- **Three dimension ( 3-D) :** Here all the dimensions are outside the nanometric range eg. Bundles of nanowires,tubes

### Classification based in materials

- **Carbon based nanomaterials :** These are defined as materials in which the nanocomponent is a pure carbon eg : carbon nano tubes, wires, spheres etc

- **Metal based nanomaterials:** Metal based nanomaterials are materilas made of metallic nanoparticles like gold, silver etc For eg Titanium dioxide

- **Nanocomposites:** Composite nanomaterials contain a mixture of simple nanoparticles or compounds such as nanosized clays within a bulk material. The nanoparticles give better physical, mechanical, and/or chemical properties to the initial bulk material.

- **Nano polymers or Dendrimers:** Dendrimers are nanosized polymers built from branched units. These are tree-like molecules with defined cavities. They can be functionalized at the surface and can hide molecules in their cavities. A direct application of dendrimers is for drug delivery.

- **Biological nanomaterials:** These nanomaterials are of biological origin and are used for nanotechnological applications. The important features of these particles are i) self assembly properties and ii) specific molecular recognition eg. DNA nano particles, nanostructured peptides. Various self assembled peptide structures can be designed to release compounds under specific conditions and are used in drug delivery systems.

## CARBON NANOTUBES

### Structure

- Carbon nanotubes are allotropes of carbon with cylindrical nanostructures. They are graphene sheet rolled into a tube.

- Carbon nanotubes are one-dimensional nano particles with diameter measuring 1-50 nm and are generally only a few micrometres in length. They are molecular scale tubes and are nanoscopic hollow fibres of pure carbon.

- CNTs are at least 100 times stronger than steel, but only one-sixth as dense. In addition, they conduct heat and electricity far better than copper.

- **Classification :** Single-walled nanotubes (SWNTs) are cylindrical structures made from a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice, while multi-walled nanotubes (MWNTs) consist of multiple concentric layers of carbon atoms.

### Properties Of Carbon Nanotubes:

***Strength and hardness:*** They are the strongest and stiffest materials. Single walled nanotubes (SWNTs) are used for synthesizing super-hard material. Multi walled nanotubes (MWNT) without inter connected inner shells exhibit telescoping property.

***Electrical conductivity:*** Their electrical conductivity is better than metals.MWNTs with interconnected inner shells show superconductivity with relatively high temperatures.

<!-- page 30 | Application Of Carbon Nanotubes: -->

***Thermal conductivity:*** Their thermal conductivity also is better than metals. The thermal stability of carbon nanotubes is found to be upto $2800^{\circ}\text{C}$ in vacuum and about $750^{\circ}\text{C}$ in air.

### Application Of Carbon Nanotubes:

1. ***Electrical circuits:*** Nanotube based transistors have been made that operates at room temperature. Carbon nanotubes are used for miniaturizing electronic devices.

2. ***Energy storage:*** Due to high surface area, optimised electrical and thermal properties they are widely employed in energy storage applications.

(a) Paper Batteries: A paper battery is a battery engineered to use a paper-thin sheet of cellulose infused with aligned carbon nanotubes. It gives a steady power output. This battery also functions as a super-capacitor which give a quick explode of high energy.

(b) Solar Cells: CNT-fullerene hybrid solar cell is formed by a mixture of carbon nan- otubes and fullerenes. Electrons trapped inside fullerenes are excited by sunlight leading to the flow of this electrons, which produce the current. CNT acts as the conductive pathway.

(c) They are used for making ultra-capacitors which provide a large surface to store electrical charge

3. ***Aerospace and automotive industry:*** Due to easier molding, high strength, stiffness, and reduced weight, CNT polymer composites are preferred for aerospace and automo- tive applications.
