## Nanomaterials and Synthesis

Bridge: Having understood general material science, we now step down to the nanometer scale to study quantum and surface phenomena.

The new idea: Materials exhibit drastically altered properties when reduced to dimensions below $100\text{ nm}$, and can be synthesized via controlled chemical precipitation and sol-gel techniques.

### Introduction to Nanomaterials

Nanomaterials are defined as materials having components with a size less than $100\text{ nm}$ at least in one dimension. They can comprise metals, ceramics, polymeric materials, or composite materials.

**Nanotechnology**

Nanotechnology is the study of manipulating matter on an atomic and molecular scale. Generally, nanotechnology deals with structures sized between $1$ to $100\text{ nanometers}$ in at least one dimension, and involves developing materials or devices within that size. The term "nano" means a billionth ($1 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$ or $1\text{ nm}$).

### Classification of Nanomaterials

#### Classification Based on Dimensions
Nanomaterials are classified into four types based on the number of dimensions that are not confined to the nanoscale range ($\le 100\text{ nm}$):

| S. No. | Dimensional class | Examples |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0-dimensional | Nanoclusters, nanoparticles |
| 2 | 1-dimensional | Nanotubes, nanowires |
| 3 | 2-dimensional | Thin films, surface coatings |
| 4 | 3-dimensional | Precipitates, colloids |

1. **Zero dimension (0-D):** All three dimensions are in the nanometric range. Examples include nanoclusters and nanoparticles.
2. **One dimension (1-D):** One of the three dimensions is outside the nanometric range while the other two are within the range. Examples include nanotubes, nanowires, and fibers.
3. **Two dimensions (2-D):** Two dimensions are outside the nanometric range and one is within the range. Examples include nanofilms, layers, and coatings.
4. **Three dimensions (3-D):** All dimensions are outside the nanometric range. Examples include bulk powders, precipitates, colloids, bundles of nanowires and tubes, and multi-nanolayers.

#### Classification Based on Materials
1. **Carbon-based nanomaterials:** Materials in which the nanocomponent is pure carbon. Examples include carbon nanotubes (CNTs), wires, and spheres (fullerenes).
2. **Metal-based nanomaterials:** Materials made of metallic nanoparticles such as gold, silver, and metal oxides like $\text{TiO}_2$.
3. **Nanocomposites:** Composite nanomaterials containing a mixture of simple nanoparticles or compounds, such as nanosized clays within a bulk material. The nanoparticles impart enhanced physical, mechanical, and chemical properties to the initial bulk material.
4. **Nanopolymers or Dendrimers:** Dendrimers are nanosized polymers built from branched units. These are tree-like molecules with defined cavities that can be functionalized at the surface and can encapsulate molecules within their cavities. A direct application of dendrimers is in targeted drug delivery.
5. **Biological nanomaterials:** Nanomaterials of biological origin utilized for nanotechnological applications, characterized by self-assembly properties and specific molecular recognition. Examples include DNA nanoparticles and nanostructured peptides.

### Synthesis of Nanomaterials

#### 1. Hydrolysis and Sol-Gel Process

Nanoparticles of metal oxides can be prepared by the hydrolysis of their alkoxide solutions under controlled conditions. Commercially important nanoparticles of silica (\(\text{SiO}_2\)), titania (\(\text{TiO}_2\)), and alumina (\(\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\)) are prepared by this method.

$$\text{Ti(OR)}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{TiO}_2 + 4\text{ROH}$$

**Sol-Gel Process**

Sol-Gel Process is this technique based on the phase transformation of a sol into a gel. Hydrolysis of metallic alkoxides or metal salts yields a sol at a suitable temperature and pH. Since the sol contains impurities, it is transformed into a gel by altering factors such as pH. The resulting gel is purified by filtration and washing with suitable solvents. Drying the purified gel produces solid nanoparticles.

For instance, aluminium nanoparticles are obtained via the hydrolysis of aluminium alkoxide using the sol-gel technique:

$$2\text{Al(OR)}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{ROH}$$

#### 2. Chemical Reduction Method

Nanoparticles of noble metals like gold and silver can be prepared by reducing their respective salt solutions using suitable reducing agents such as sodium borohydride (\(\text{NaBH}_4\)), glucose, or trisodium citrate.

**Reduction using reducing agents**

Reduction using reducing agents produces nanoparticles of molybdenum from \(\text{MoCl}_2\) in a toluene solution using \(\text{NaBH(C}_2\text{H}_5)_3\) as a reducing agent at room temperature:

$$3\text{MoCl}_2 + 6\text{NaBH(C}_2\text{H}_5)_3 \rightarrow 3\text{Mo} + 6\text{NaCl} + 3\text{H}_2 + 6\text{B(C}_2\text{H}_5)_3$$

Silver nanoparticles are synthesized by boiling an \(\text{AgNO}_3\) solution and adding trisodium citrate drop by drop with vigorous stirring using a magnetic stirrer. The mixture is then cooled and allowed to rest until the color changes to a light green. Finally, the solvent is removed to recover the silver nanoparticles.

### Applications of Nanomaterials

Nanomaterials are widely implemented in manufacturing processes, consumer products, and healthcare systems, including paints, filters, insulation, and lubricant additives. In healthcare, notable examples include nanozymes—nanomaterials exhibiting intrinsic enzyme-like characteristics.

## Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs)

Building on general nanomaterials, we examine carbon nanotubes as specialized 1D structures with exceptional strength and conductivity. Rolling graphene sheets into cylindrical tubes creates carbon nanotubes with unique chiral orientations and ultra-high tensile strength.

Carbon is an element with four well-known allotropic forms: diamond, graphite, fullerene, and carbon nanotubes. Carbon nanotubes (CNTs), originally referred to as Bucky tubes, are one-dimensional nanoparticles characterized by tube-like structures with diameters ranging from approximately $1\text{ nm}$ to $30\text{ nm}$ and lengths on the order of millimeters. All atoms in CNTs are carbon. They are approximately $10^5$ times thinner than a human hair and at least $100$ times stronger than steel.

### Classification of CNTs

Depending on the number of walls, carbon nanotubes are categorized into two main types: Single-Walled Carbon Nanotubes (SWCNTs) and Multi-Walled Carbon Nanotubes (MWCNTs).

#### Single-Walled Carbon Nanotubes (SWCNTs)

**Single-Walled Carbon Nanotubes**

An SWCNT consists of only a single layer constructed by rolling up a one-atom-thick sheet of graphite (graphene) into a cylinder. The diameter of an SWCNT typically varies from $1\text{ nm}$ to $5\text{ nm}$.

![Armchair, zigzag, and chiral carbon nanotube structures](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/cnt.webp)

| Nanotube type | Graphene wall structure |
| --- | --- |
| SWCNT | One graphene sheet rolled into a single cylindrical wall. |
| MWCNT | Several concentric graphene cylinders form multiple walls. |
SWCNTs can be visualized as rolled-up tubular shells of a graphene sheet. Based on variations arising from specific orientations and rolling angles of the graphene sheets, SWCNTs are classified into three distinct types:

1. **Armchair Nanotubes:** Exhibit an arrangement where the rolling axis aligns with the hexagonal lattice of graphene, resulting in equal edge lengths.
2. **Zigzag Nanotubes:** Formed when the rolling axis is along the zigzag pattern of the graphene lattice, leading to edges consisting solely of zigzag lines.
3. **Chiral Nanotubes:** Characterized by a rolling axis oriented at an angle to the graphene lattice, resulting in unequal edge lengths and a helical structure.

#### Multi-Walled Carbon Nanotubes (MWCNTs)

**Multi-Walled Carbon Nanotubes**

An MWCNT consists of a stack of graphene sheets rolled up into concentric cylinders, resembling the growth rings of a tree trunk. They contain multiple concentric nanotube cylinders constructed one inside the other. The inner diameter of MWCNTs varies from $1.5\text{ nm}$ to $5\text{ nm}$ and the outer diameter varies from $2.5\text{ nm}$ to $30\text{ nm}$. Generally, SWCNTs are more stable and exhibit better-defined cylindrical shapes than MWCNTs.

![MWCNT nested atomic model](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/p005-2-gemini.webp)

Three different structural models are proposed for MWCNTs:

- **Russian Doll Model:** Sheets of graphene are arranged into concentric cylinders.
- **Parchment Model:** A single graphene sheet is rolled around itself multiple times, similar to a scroll of parchment.
- **Mixed Model:** A combination of both the Russian doll and parchment models.

### Properties of CNTs

1. **Electrical Conductivity:** They are highly conducting and can behave as metallic or semiconducting materials depending on their chirality and diameter.
2. **Thermal Conductivity:** They exhibit very high thermal conductivity and kinetic properties.
3. **Chemical Inertness:** CNTs are chemically very inert and difficult to oxidize.

### Applications of CNTs

1. **Shielding Materials:** Used as lightweight shielding materials for protecting sensitive electronic equipment.
2. **Catalysts:** Act as efficient catalyst supports for various chemical reactions.
3. **Water Treatment:** Used as a microscopic filter medium in water softening and filtration processes.
4. **Electrical Circuits:** Employed in nanotube-based field-effect transistors operating at room temperature, facilitating the miniaturization of electronic devices.
5. **Energy Storage:** Due to high surface area and optimized electrical and thermal properties:
   - **Paper Batteries:** Engineered using a paper-thin sheet of cellulose infused with aligned carbon nanotubes, delivering a steady power output and functioning simultaneously as a supercapacitor for high-energy bursts.
   - **Solar Cells:** CNT-fullerene hybrid solar cells use a mixture where electrons trapped inside fullerenes are excited by sunlight to produce a current, with CNTs acting as the conductive pathways.
   - **Ultra-Capacitors:** Used to create ultra-capacitors providing large surface areas to store electrical charges.
6. **Aerospace and Automotive Industry:** CNT polymer composites are heavily utilized due to easier molding, high strength, high stiffness, and reduced weight.
7. **Hydrogen Storage:** Research is ongoing to assess the ability of CNTs to safely store hydrogen.

## Fullerenes

Fullerenes are zero-dimensional, hollow, closed-cage nanoparticles composed entirely of carbon atoms. They consist of fused hexagonal rings and alternating pentagonal rings of carbon closed in spherical or ellipsoidal geometries.

| Fullerene | Cage structure |
| --- | --- |
| C₆₀ buckminsterfullerene | Truncated icosahedral cage with 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons |
| C₂₀ fullerene | Dodecahedral cage with 12 pentagons |
Fullerene molecules are denoted by the number of carbon atoms in the spherical carbon cage. The smallest fullerene reported is $C_{20}$, followed by $C_{24}$, $C_{28}$, and higher analogues. Each fullerene generally contains $12$ pentagonal rings and $\left(\frac{n}{2} - 10\right)$ hexagonal rings, where $n \ge 20$ represents the total number of carbon atoms.

$C_{60}$ fullerene, commonly known as Buckminsterfullerene (or buckyball), is the earliest discovered and most widely studied form of fullerene. In 1990, a scalable production technique was developed by heating graphite rods in a helium atmosphere. $C_{60}$ is composed of fused pentagonal and hexagonal carbon rings ($12$ five-membered rings and $20$ six-membered rings) and possesses a perfect icosahedral symmetry structurally identical to a soccer football.

## Fullerenes and Graphene

Bridge: From 1D nanotubes, we expand to zero-dimensional closed cages (fullerenes) and two-dimensional single atomic sheets (graphene).

The new idea: Graphene serves as the fundamental 2D building block of all graphitic materials, exhibiting massless relativistic charge carriers and extreme mechanical flexibility.

### Fullerenes ($C_{60}$)

Fullerenes are stable, but not totally unreactive. The $\text{sp}^2$ hybridized carbon atoms, which are at their energy minimum in planar graphite, must be bent to form the closed sphere or tube, which produces angle strain. The characteristic reaction of fullerenes is electrophilic addition at 6,6-double bonds, which reduces angle strain by changing $\text{sp}^2$ hybridized carbons into $\text{sp}^3$ hybridized ones. The change in hybridized orbitals causes the bond angles to decrease from about $120^\circ$ in the $\text{sp}^2$ orbitals to about $109.5^\circ$ in the $\text{sp}^3$ orbitals. This decrease in bond angles allows for the bonds to bend less when closing the sphere or tube, and thus, the molecule becomes more stable.

#### Properties of Fullerenes

1. **Solubility**

   Fullerenes are sparingly soluble in many solvents but soluble in toluene and $\text{CS}_2$.

2. **Colour**

   - Fullerene ($\text{C}_{60}$) is a fine black powder.
   - Solutions of $\text{C}_{70}$ are reddish-brown in colour.
   - The higher fullerenes $\text{C}_{76}$ to $\text{C}_{84}$ have a variety of colours.

3. **Conductivity**

   Fullerenes are electrical insulators as there is no movement of electrons available from one molecule to another. However, when crystallized with alkali metals, the resultant compound can be conducting or even superconducting.

4. **Hydrogenation**

   As $\text{C}_{60}$ exhibits a small degree of aromatic character, it undergoes addition reactions with hydrogen.

5. **Halogenation**

   $\text{C}_{60}$ undergoes halogenation with F, Cl, and Br under a variety of conditions.

6. **Physical Strength**

   Fullerenes are extremely strong molecules, able to resist great pressures and will bounce back to their original shape after being subjected to over 3,000 atmospheres. They can withstand collisions of up to 15,000 mph against stainless steel, demonstrating exceptionally high stability.

7. **Light Absorption**

   Fullerenes absorb strongly in the UV region and moderately in the visible regions of the spectrum.

8. **Chemical Reactivity**

   The carbon atoms within a fullerene molecule are $\text{sp}^2$ and $\text{sp}^3$ hybridized, of which the $\text{sp}^2$ carbons are responsible for the considerable angle strain present. $\text{C}_{60}$ and $\text{C}_{70}$ exhibit the capacity to be reversibly reduced with up to six electrons.

#### Applications of Fullerenes

1. **Coatings and Lubricants**

   Used for making durable, high-performance coatings that resist wear and corrosion, and as miniature 'ball bearings' or catalysts to lubricate surfaces.

2. **Biomedical**

   Employed to deliver drugs to specific cells or tissues due to stable complex formation, and as contrast agents in imaging techniques such as MRI or ultrasound.

3. **Energy Storage**

   Employed in energy storage devices due to electrical conductivity and stability, including hydrogen storage.

4. **Environmental**

   Applied in water treatment for the removal of pollutants, and in sensors for detecting environmental toxins.

## Graphene

Graphene is an allotrope of carbon consisting of a monolayer of carbon atoms arranged in a two-dimensional honeycomb lattice. It can be visualized as a single layer extracted from the layered structure of graphite. Graphite is three-dimensional whereas graphene is two-dimensional with a thickness of one atom.

![Graphite and Graphene structure comparison](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/p009-1-gemini.webp)

In graphene, the carbon atoms are $\text{sp}^2$ hybridized and arranged in a hexagonal fashion. Each hexagonal ring comprises three strong in-plane $\sigma$ bonds, with a bond length of $0.142\text{ nm}$, and a $p_z$ orbital perpendicular to the plane. These orbitals hybridize together to form two half-filled bands of free-moving electrons, $\pi$ and $\pi^*$, which are responsible for most of graphene's notable electronic properties.

### Properties of Graphene

- **Electrical Conductivity**: Graphene has high electrical conductivity and exhibits the Quantum Hall effect. Electrons behave as massless relativistic particles (Dirac fermions), which allows electron speeds comparable to light and high electron mobility compared to metals.
- **Mechanical Strength and Elasticity**: Possesses high elastic modulus and strength (200 times stronger than steel). It is highly flexible and can be stretched by up to $20\%$ of its original length without structural damage.
- **Thermal Conductivity**: Exhibits excellent thermal conductivity, making it highly efficient for heat dissipation and thermal management applications.
- **Optical Transparency**: Absorbs only $2.3\%$ of incident light over a broad wavelength range, making it suitable for transparent electrodes in displays, solar cells, and touchscreens.
- **Surface Area**: Features an extremely high surface area due to its single-atom thickness, highly advantageous for energy storage.

### Applications of Graphene

1. **Electronics**: Graphene-based thin films provide lower resistance and higher transparency than indium tin oxide for touchscreens; enables smaller, higher-performance transistors.
2. **Energy Storage**: Incorporated into lithium-ion batteries for longer lifespans, faster charging times, and higher capacity; improves hydrogen fuel cell efficiency by lowering fuel crossover; used in supercapacitors for high energy density.
3. **Biomedical**: Functionalized graphene carries chemotherapy drugs to cancer cells; used in highly sensitive biosensors for detecting DNA, ATP, and dopamine.
4. **Composites and Coatings**: Prevents rusting in paints; embedded in aircraft wing carbon-fiber coatings to resist impact and reduce fuel consumption; improves sports equipment like skis and bicycles.
5. **Environmental**: Water purification membranes and air filters/coatings to remove pollutants and toxins.

# Carbon Quantum Dots (CQDs)

Carbon quantum dots are zero-dimensional small carbon nanoparticles consisting of ultra-fine, distributed, quasi-spherical carbon structures with sizes less than $10\text{ nm}$. CQDs are superior to traditional semiconductor quantum dots due to lower molecular weight, reduced toxicity, ease of surface functionalization, cost-effectiveness, exceptional fluorescence stability, tunable emission wavelengths, and strong biocompatibility.

Synthesis routes include the 'top-down' approach (breaking down large carbon structures via chemical oxidation, laser ablation, or arc discharge) and the 'bottom-up' approach (building from small carbon molecules via hydrothermal or microwave-assisted synthesis).

### Properties of CQDs

- **Optical**: Absorption mainly in the UV region extending into the visible region. They exhibit strong fluorescence, photoluminescence, and chemiluminescence.
- **Stability and Biocompatibility**: Excellent photostability and biocompatibility with minimal toxicity compared to other quantum dots.

### Applications of CQDs

1. **Biomedical**: Employed in fluorescent bioimaging and targeted drug delivery systems.

# Polymers

A polymer may consist of monomers of identical or different chemical structures, known as homopolymers or copolymers respectively. When a polymer is made by linking together only one type of small molecule, or monomer, it is called a homopolymer. Examples include Polyethylene, Polypropylene, and PVC. When two different types of monomers are joined in the same polymer chain, the polymer is called a copolymer, such as butadiene-styrene (BS) and Acrylonitrile butadiene-styrene (ABS). Two special classes of polymers viz., **fire-retardant polymers** and **conducting polymers** are discussed here.

### Fire-retardant Polymers

Most of the polymers contain organic compounds as monomers and are flammable. **Fire-retardant polymers resist ignition and slow the spread of flames.** Phenolic polymers such as novolac and bakelite, as well as aromatic heterocyclic polyimides and polybenzoxazoles, are examples of intrinsically fire-resistant polymers.

![Examples for non-halogenated fire-retardant polymers](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/p011-1-gemini.webp)
Figure 2.4: Examples for non-halogenated fire-retardant polymers. Shows a chemical reaction pathway where 'n' molecules of Methylol phenol link to form 'Novolac' via a condensation reaction releasing water molecules. Below Novolac, the complex 2D cross-linked chemical structure of 'Bakelite' is shown with alternating phenolic rings connected by $CH_2$ bridges and pendant OH groups.

Halogenated polymers are also fire-retardant polymers. Fire is a free radical chain reaction system which contains radicals such as $\text{H}^{\bullet}$, $\text{OH}^{\bullet}$, $\text{OOH}^{\bullet}$, and $\text{CH}^{\bullet}_3$. In the case of halogenated polymers like PVC and polybrominated styrene, halogen radicals ($\text{Cl}^{\bullet}$, $\text{Br}^{\bullet}$) are introduced into the fire, which combine with hydrogen and hydroxyl radicals to form stable molecules like $\text{HX}$ and $\text{HOX}$ (where $\text{X}$ can be $\text{Cl}$ or $\text{Br}$), resulting in quenching of the chain reaction.

Among halogens, bromine is found to be more effective in quenching fire. However, the use of halogenated polymers is not heavily recommended since they remain in the environment for long periods and can release toxic by-products during combustion or degradation.

# Conducting Polymers

**Conducting polymers**

An organic polymer which can conduct electricity is called a conducting polymer. Examples include polyacetylenes, polyanilines, and polypyrroles. Broadly, they are categorized into:

### 1. Intrinsically Conducting Polymers
This type of polymer has extensive conjugation in the backbone (continuous $\text{Sp}^2$ hybridized carbon centers) which is responsible for conductance. These are subdivided into two:

(i) **Conducting polymers having conjugated $\pi$-electrons in the backbone:** Here, the overlapping of conjugated $\pi$-electrons over the entire backbone results in the formation of valence bands as well as conduction bands, which extend over the entire polymer molecule. But since the valence band and conduction band are separated by a significant band gap, electrical conduction can occur only after thermal or photolytic activation of electrons to give them sufficient energy to cross the gap. Conductivity of these polymers alone is typically not sufficient for various applications.

(ii) **Doped conducting polymers:** Here, conductivities of polymers having conjugated $\pi$-electrons in the backbone are increased by creating positive or negative charges on the polymer backbone—i.e., by oxidative doping or reductive doping.

### 2. Extrinsically Conducting Polymers
These polymers owe their conductivity to the presence of externally added ingredients in them. They are categorized into two:

(i) **Conductive element filled polymer:** Here, the polymer acts as a binder to hold conducting elements such as carbon black, metallic fibers, and metallic oxides. Special conducting-grade carbon black is used for this purpose.

(ii) **Blended conducting polymers:** These polymers are made by blending a conventional polymer with a conducting polymer.

### Doping Mechanisms

To become electrically conductive, the electrons of the polymer need to be free to move. The polymer having conjugated double bonds must be disturbed either by removing electrons from or inserting electrons into the polymer by oxidation or reduction. This process is known as doping.

#### Types of Doping:
1. **p-doping**

   Oxidation with Lewis acids or halogens. In p-doping, an intrinsically conducting polymer or a polymer having conjugated $\text{C}=\text{C}$ bonds is treated with Lewis acids or halogens, which oxidizes the polymer and creates positively charged sites on the polymer backbone. Due to the positive charge, they act as good conductors of electricity similar to metals.
   * *Examples for p-dopant:* $\text{I}_2, \text{Br}_2, \text{AlCl}_3, \text{AsF}_5, \text{FeCl}_3$.

2. **n-doping**

   Reduction with Lewis bases or alkali metals. In n-doping, an intrinsically conducting polymer or a polymer having conjugated $\text{C}=\text{C}$ bonds is treated with Lewis bases or alkali metals, which reduces the polymer and creates negatively charged sites on the polymer backbone. Due to the negative charge, they act as good conductors of electricity.
   * *Examples for n-dopant:* $\text{Na}, \text{K}, \text{Lithium naphthalides}$, etc.

## Polyaniline, Polypyrrole, and OLEDs

Bridge: With the fundamentals of conducting polymers established, we apply them to practical electronic devices like polyaniline electrodes and OLED displays.

The new idea: Organic light-emitting diodes generate radiation through the recombination of injected electrons and holes inside an organic semiconductor layer.

### Polyaniline

**Preparation**

Polyaniline is a conjugated polymer considered as an organic metal. Its conductivity and thermal dependence of conductivity are like those of semi-metals, but all other properties are like those of metals. It is prepared by the slow addition of an aqueous solution of ammonium persulphate to a solution of aniline in aqueous $\text{HCl}$ at a lower temperature ($0\text{-}5^\circ\text{C}$). The precipitate formed is separated by filtration, which is emeraldine hydrochloride.

![Preparation reaction of Polyaniline from Aniline and Ammonium persulphate](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/p014-1-gemini.webp)
Polyaniline is a transparent and stable material. It shows a green colour in the conducting state. It turns red under reducing conditions and blue under oxidizing conditions.

#### Properties
1. It is a lightweight, highly flexible material.
2. It possesses very high chemical resistance.
3. It has a wide and controllable range of conductivity.
4. It can be processed into any shape by using ordinary fabrication methods.

#### Applications
1. As a shield in complex electronic circuits against electromagnetic radiations.
2. As electrode materials in rechargeable batteries.
3. In biosensors and solar energy absorption.

### Polypyrrole

Polypyrrole is an organic polymer formed by the polymerization of pyrrole. Polymerization of pyrrole may be carried out chemically or electrochemically. It is a very good conducting polymer with conjugated double bonds. Conducting polypyrrole consists of a number of connected pyrrole ring structures.

**Preparation**

Polypyrrole is prepared by oxidation (p-doping) of pyrrole using ferric chloride in methanol.

![Preparation reaction of Polypyrrole from Pyrrole and Ferric chloride](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/p014-2-gemini.webp)

The polymerization and p-doping can also be carried out electrochemically.

#### Properties
1. Polypyrrole films are yellow in colour but darken in air due to oxidation.
2. Undoped and doped films are insoluble in common solvents but are swellable.
3. They are stable in air up to $150^\circ\text{C}$; at this temperature, the dopant starts to evolve.
4. Polypyrrole is an insulator, but its oxidized derivatives are good electrical conductors.

#### Applications
1. Used in biosensors and immunosensors because of its biocompatibility.
2. Used in tissue engineering.
3. Employed as a counter electrode in dye-sensitized solar cells.

### OLED (Organic Light Emitting Diode)

An organic light emitting diode (OLED) is a light-emitting diode in which the emissive electroluminescent layer is a film of an organic compound that emits light in response to an electric current. An organic semiconductor is situated between two electrodes: the anode and cathode. The HOMO (Highest Occupied Molecular Orbital) and the LUMO (Lowest Unoccupied Molecular Orbital) of organic semiconductors are analogous to the valence and conduction bands of inorganic semiconductors.

![OLED layers and electron-hole recombination](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/oled.webp)

#### Structure
A typical OLED consists of the following parts:
1. **Substrate:** Supports the OLED (e.g., clear plastic, glass, foil, etc.).
2. **Anode:** Removes electrons when a current flows through the device (e.g., Indium Tin Oxide, ITO).
3. **Hole Transport Layer (HTL):** A conducting polymer layer that transports holes from the anode (e.g., polyaniline).
4. **Electron Transport Layer (ETL):** The emissive layer made of an organic molecule where light is produced (e.g., polyfluorene).
5. **Cathode:** Injects electrons when a current flows through the device (e.g., Ag).

#### Working Principle
During operation, a voltage is applied across the OLED such that the anode is positive with respect to the cathode. A current of electrons flows through the device from cathode to anode, as electrons are injected into the LUMO of the organic layer at the cathode and withdrawn from the HOMO at the anode. As a result, the electron transport layer (ETL) becomes negative and the hole transport layer (HTL) becomes positive in charge. Mobile holes can move faster than electrons; they jump across the junction and combine with electrons present in the electron transport layer (ETL). During this recombination, both cancel out and release a burst of energy in the form of radiation. This is known as hole-electron recombination, which occurs many times in a second.

#### Advantages
1. OLEDs can be printed onto any suitable substrate, making them cheaper to produce than LCD or plasma displays.
2. OLED displays can be fabricated on flexible plastic substrates, leading to flexible organic light-emitting diodes.
3. OLEDs have a much faster response time than LCDs.
4. They possess better power efficiency and a thinner profile.

#### Disadvantages
1. The biggest technical problem for OLEDs is the limited lifetime of the organic materials.
2. OLED materials used to produce blue light degrade more rapidly than materials that produce other colours.
3. Water can instantly damage the organic materials of the display.
4. OLEDs can consume $60\text{-}80\%$ more power than LCDs under certain white-display conditions.

### Dye-Sensitized Solar Cells (DSSCs)

A solar cell is an electrical device that converts the energy of light directly into electricity by the photovoltaic effect. Conventional solar cells use crystalline silicon semiconductors. A **dye-sensitized solar cell (DSSC)** is a photoelectrochemical cell that converts solar light absorbed by a dye into electric energy, operating as a thin-film solar cell.

#### Construction of Dye-Sensitized Solar Cells
A DSSC mainly consists of a substrate, a transparent conductive oxide layer (TCO), semiconductor metal oxide nanoparticles, an organic/inorganic dye, an electrolyte, and a counter electrode.
- **Substrate:** Generally made of glass or flexible transparent plastic.
- **Transparent Conductive Oxide (TCO):** A thin film of Indium Tin Oxide (ITO) or Fluorine-doped Tin Oxide (FTO) acts as the TCO.

### Dye-Sensitized Solar Cells (DSSCs)

**Semiconductor metal oxide nanoparticles**

Semiconductor metal oxide nanoparticles are semiconducting metal oxides with high surface area and wide band gaps that are used in DSSCs. $\text{TiO}_2$ is the commonly used metal oxide. Other metal oxide like $\text{ZnO}$ and $\text{SnO}_2$ are also used.

**Dye/photo sensitiser**

Dye/photo sensitiser refers to molecules where the surface of the metal oxide particle is coated with dye molecules. These dye molecules are responsible for absorbing sunlight and inject the photoexcited electrons to the conduction band of the semiconductor. Dye-sensitized metal oxide semiconductor acts as the anode. Ruthenium-based complexes and organic dyes are commonly used. Natural dyes extracted from plants can also be used as photosensitiser.

![Chemical structure of N3-dye](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/p017-1-gemini.webp)
**N719 dye composition:** a ruthenium(II) centre coordinated by two dicarboxybipyridyl ligands and two thiocyanate ($\mathrm{NCS}$) ligands. The salt form is balanced by two tetrabutylammonium ($\mathrm{TBA^+}$) counterions.

**Electrolyte**

Electrolyte is a redox couple iodate/triiodide solution used as the electrolyte medium. Electrolyte should have good contact with the dye-sensitized metal oxide semiconductor and the counter electrode.

**Counter electrode**

Counter electrode consists of platinum or graphite based materials employed as the counter electrodes, which acts as the cathode. It facilitates the reduction of the oxidized dye molecules and completes the electrical circuit.

![Dye-sensitized solar cell structure and electron flow](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/dssc.webp)

**Working of Dye-sensitized solar cells**

Working of Dye-sensitized solar cells involves sunlight passing through the transparent conductive oxide layer and the dye molecules coated on the metal oxide semiconductor absorb the light. The electrons of the dye molecule then get excited and are injected into the conduction band of the metal oxide semiconductor. These electrons travel through metal oxide and move towards the external circuit to generate electric current. Electrons reach the counter electrode through the circuit and generate electricity. The oxidized dye molecules will get reduced by gaining electrons from the electrolyte and come back to their original form. The oxidized electrolyte species diffuse towards the counter electrode and get reduced. This process continues to produce the current.

#### Advantages of DSSC
- The fabrication of DSSCs is simpler processes compared to conventional silicon solar cells.
- They are light weight which can be advantageous for applications on vehicles and portable devices.
- They are flexible in shape, and size and hence can be implemented on windows, terraces, facades, etc.
- Natural pigments and organic dyes can be employed makes them more sustainable, ecofriendly and affordable.

#### Applications of DSSC
- DSSCs are mainly used for sunlight harvesting.
- Since they can be used in window, facades etc., they can generate power while maintaining aesthetic appearance.
- They can be used in portable solar chargers for electronic devices, such as smartphones and tablets due to its lightweight.
- They can be integrated into the fabric of bags and backpacks, enabling them to charge electronic devices while on the go. This feature makes them particularly popular among hikers and travellers who need a portable and convenient power source.
- DSSC can be used to power illuminated signs and displays in locations where access to electricity is limited or costly.

### Materials in Quantum Computing Technologies

**Quantum computing**

Quantum computing is an emerging computer technology that uses the principle of quantum theory for computation. Quantum computers harness the properties such as superposition (quantum particles as combination of all possible states), entanglement (ability of quantum particles to correlate their measurement results with each other) and quantum interference (behaviour of a qubit, arising from superposition, which influences the probability of the qubit collapsing to one state or another upon measurement) for computing.

Quantum computing uses quantum bits (qubits) that are superpositions of 'spin up' and 'spin down' states, allowing them to represent all numbers between 0 and 255 simultaneously. This allows quantum computers to perform many calculations in parallel. Spintronic devices have the potential for quantum computing. The spins of the electrons are expected to play an important role in the quantum computers, as they can be used as qubits or quantum bit. Hexagonal boron nitride, a material consisting of just a few monolayers of atoms, can be stacked to serve as the insulating layer in the capacitors used in superconducting qubits. Diamonds with nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers, nanowires, including semiconductor, superconducting, and topological nanowires are also used in quantum computing. Metals like Al, Nb are used in used in superconducting circuits. Quantum dots materials like indium arsenide (InAs) and gallium arsenide (GaAs) are employed to produce single photons, which are crucial for quantum communication applications. Quantum computing requires very low temperatures to avoid decoherence so that qubits remains in their quantum states and prevents error rates.

### Supercapacitors

**Supercapacitor**

A supercapacitor is an electrochemical energy storage device, which can be used to store and deliver charge by reversible adsorption and desorption of ions at the interface between the electrode material and electrolyte. Supercapacitors are also called ultracapacitors or electrochemical capacitors.

![Supercapacitor charging and discharging](/assets/notes/ece/s1/chemistry/module-2/supercap.webp)

In supercapacitors energy is stored electrostatically and electrochemically and bridges the gap between traditional capacitors and batteries. A capacitor contains two metal plates separated by a dielectric material whereas a supercapacitor generally consists of electrodes, electrolyte, separator and collector.

#### Components of Supercapacitors
- **Electrode**: Materials with good conductivity and high stability like porous active carbon coating are generally used as the electrode material. Carbon based nanoparticles (eg. carbon nanotubes, graphene, carbon quantum dots etc.), metal oxide nanoparticles, nanowires, quantum dots, conducting polymer-based composites etc. are used as the electrode material in supercapacitors. Nanomaterials enhances the performance of the supercapacitors by increasing the capacitance, energy density, and cycling stability of the supercapacitors.
- **Electrolyte**: Capacity of the capacitor depends mainly on the dielectric constant of the electrolyte. Either solid or liquid can be used as the electrolyte. For commercial application solid electrolytes are preferred since they are leak-free and possess high ionic conductivity. Generally, a solvent mixed with conductive salts such as tetradkylammonium or lithium salts acts as the solid electrolyte. Sulphuric acid, KOH solution etc can act as the liquid electrolyte.
- **Separator**: Electrolyte membranes will act as the separator. It prevents short-circuiting between the electrodes but allows the electrolyte ions to pass through. Polymer-based and paper-based are found to be durable and economical.
- **Collector**: Electrons are collected at the collector. Carbon fibre, metal like Al, Pt, Cu, Ni etc. are the commonly used collectors. Binders, such as polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) and poly-acrylonitrile (PAN), act as adhesives to hold the active material and current collectors together.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages of Supercapacitors
- **Advantages**: Longer cycling time, high efficiency, high power density, low resistance, small size/lightweight, and environmentally friendly.
- **Disadvantages**: Higher self-discharge rate, lower energy storage per unit weight compared to batteries, and low voltage limits.

#### Applications of Supercapacitors
- Used in hybrid buses to increase battery life and decrease size.
- Implantable devices and health monitoring devices.
- Solar-powered road and farming devices, street lights, and portable traffic lights.
- Wind turbines to supply power to pitch control mechanisms.
- Memory backup devices in laptops, smartphones, and tablets.

### Spintronics

**Spintronics**

Spintronics or spin-electronics is an emerging field that exploits the spin of electrons, in addition to charge, to process information in new generation transistors, lasers and integrated magnetic sensors. They act by storing information in electron spins, transmitting this information via mobile electrons and reading it at a terminal. Spin orientation lasts longer than charge-based information, making them suitable for memory storage, magnetic sensors and quantum computing.

The discovery of giant magnetoresistance (GMR) has a crucial role in the development of spintronics. GMR is a phenomenon where a material's electrical resistance changes dramatically when exposed to a magnetic field. This effect is widely used in magnetic storage (eg. Magnetic random-access memory) and sensing technologies. Giant magnetoresistive spin-valvesensor have improved the capacity of hard disc drive by more than hundred times. This sensor comprises a metallic stack of ultra-thin ferromagnetic layers separated by a thin copper layer.

A spin polarised current can be generated in a very simple way by passing the current through a ferroelectric material. Spintronics materials should retain ferromagnetism practical temperatures. There are metal based, semiconductor based and graphene based spintronics. Metal oxides such as $\text{MgO}$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{SrTiO}_3$ etc are examples of metal based spintronics materials.

Terary alloys such as InAs and GaAs alloyed with Mn, $(\text{Co}, \text{Fe}, \text{B})$, $(\text{Ga}, \text{Fe})\text{N}$ etc are used as semiconductor based spintronic materials due to their tunable magnetic and electronic properties. Graphenes are also used for development of spintronics due to its efficient spin manipulation properties.
