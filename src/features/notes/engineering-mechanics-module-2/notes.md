Centroid of Area

Centroid of an area is the geometric centre of the area where the entire area can be assumed to be concentrated.



> **Figure: Coordinate axes with an irregular area showing centroid G and its coordinates**
>
> An irregular shaped area on a 2D Cartesian coordinate system with x and y axes. Inside the area, point G is marked as G(x̄, ȳ). Dimension lines indicate the horizontal distance x̄ from the y-axis and the vertical distance ȳ from the x-axis.



$$G(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$$

The position of $G$ can be located by specifying its coordinates w.r.t $x$-$y$ reference lines. The distance of centroid from $y$-axis is denoted by $\bar{x}$ and that from the $x$ axis is denoted by $\bar{y}$.

Centre of gravity

Centre of gravity of a body is the geometric centre of the body where the entire weight is assumed to be concentrated.

Expression for $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{y}$



> **Figure: Irregular area divided into sub-areas a1, a2, a3 with corresponding coordinates**

> An irregular area divided into smaller areas a1, a2, a3 with their respective centroids marked, showing horizontal distances x1, x2, x3 and vertical distances y1, y2, y3 from the reference y and x axes.



$$\bar{x} = \frac{a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 + a_3 x_3 + \dots}{a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots}$$

$$\bar{x} = \frac{\int ax}{\int a}$$

$$\bar{y} = \frac{a_1 y_1 + a_2 y_2 + a_3 y_3 + \dots}{a_2} = \frac{\int ay}{\int a}$$

~~Mass~~ Moment of Inertia

Moment of inertia is the resistance of an object to change its rotational velocity. It is also known as rotational inertia.

Moment of inertia of a body about a particular axis depends upon both the mass of the body and its distribution relative to the axis.

Parallel axis theorem & perpendicular axis theorem

Perpendicular axis theorem

If $I_{xx}$ and $I_{yy}$ are the moment of inertia of a lamina about two

mutually perpendicular axis, $xx$ and $yy$, then moment of inertia of the lamina about $zz$ which is perpendicular to $xx$ and $yy$ axis and passing through the point of intersection of $xx$ and $yy$ axis is given by the equation

$$\boxed{I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}}$$

For example, in a rectangular lamina,

$$I_{xx} = \frac{bd^3}{12} \qquad I_{yy} = \frac{db^3}{12}$$

$$\therefore I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$$

$$I_{zz} = \frac{bd^3}{12} + \frac{db^3}{12}$$

Polar moment of inertia

The moment of inertia about an axis perpendicular to the plane of the area is called the polar moment of inertia. It is denoted by $I_z$.

where, $I_z = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$

Mass moment of inertia

The mass moment of inertia of a body is defined as the product of the mass and the square of the distance between the mass centre of the body and its axis. Mass moment of inertia of this elementary mass about AA axis is given by

$dI = r^2 dm$

## Radius of gyration

We know, $I = m \int r^2$

Substituting: $\int r^2 = k^2$

$I = m k^2$

$k^2 = \frac{I}{m}$

or $k = \sqrt{\frac{I}{m}}$

where $K \rightarrow$ radius of gyration

Unit of $K$ is $m$
