## PUMPS

- The pump is a hydraulic machine, used to converts the mechanical energy into pressure energy.
- Two types of commonly used pumps are:
  - Reciprocating pump
  - Centrifugal pump

## RECIPROCATING PUMP

- A reciprocating pump is a type of positive displacement pump that uses a reciprocating component (i.e., piston or plunger) to pump the fluid from one area to another.
- Reciprocating Pump is hydraulic machine which converts the mechanical energy into pressure energy by means of movement of reciprocating piston.
- The schematic diagram of the reciprocating pump is given below:

### Components

- The major components of reciprocating pumps are:
  1. Suction Pipe
  2. Suction Valve
  3. Delivery Pipe
  4. Delivery Valve
  5. Cylinder
  6. Piston
  7. Connecting rod
  8. Crankshaft

**Suction Pipe: -** The suction pipe is used to introduce fluid from the fluid reservoir to the pump cylinder. One end of suction pipe is dipped in the fluid reservoir (e.g., water reservoir) and other end attached to the inlet of the cylinder.

**Suction Valve: -** The suction valve is located in between the cylinder and the suction pipe. It is a non-return value.

**Delivery Pipe: -** The delivery pipe is used to deliver fluid from the pump cylinder to the desired height. One end of delivery pipe attached to the outlet of the cylinder and other end at discharge point.

**Delivery Valve: -** The delivery valve is located in between the cylinder and the delivery pipe. It is a non-return value.

**Cylinder: -** The piston is installed in the cylinder and piston reciprocates inside the cylinder to compress the fluid. The suction valve is connected to one side of the cylinder while the delivery valve is attached to the other side of the cylinder.

**Piston: -** The piston moves back and forth inside the cylinder to suck and compress the fluid. When the piston moves backward, it sucks fluid inside the pump cylinder, and when it moves forward, it pressurizes the fluid and discharges it.

**Connecting Rod: -** One end of the connecting rod is attached to the piston, while the other end is connected to the crank. It transforms the linear motion of the crank into the reciprocating motion of the piston.

**Crankshaft: -** The crankshaft is directly attached to an electrical motor. When the electric motor turns on, it rotates the crankshaft, which further reciprocates the piston.

<u>**Working**</u>

The reciprocating pump works on the positive displacement principle. A reciprocating pump contains a piston or plunger that moves back and forth inside the cylinder. The piston is connected to a crankshaft with the help of a connecting rod. This piston moves according to the movement of the connecting rod. The connecting rod gets power from the crankshaft and the crankshaft is connected to an electric motor.

The pump cylinder is connected to a suction pipe and a delivery pipe with a suction valve and a delivery valve. The suction and delivery valves allow the fluid to flow in one direction. The fluid sucks into the cylinder through the inlet valve and the fluid exits the cylinder of the pump through the outlet valve. When the piston moves backward, it sucks fluid inside the pump cylinder, and when it moves forward, it pressurizes the fluid and delivered to the desired location or storage tank.

## CENTRIFUGAL PUMP

- Centrifugal Pump is hydraulic machine which converts the mechanical energy into pressure energy by means of centrifugal force acting on the fluid.
- It is one of the most common types of pumps used today's world.
- The schematic diagram of the centrifugal pump is given below:



> **Figure: Schematic diagram of a centrifugal pump showing its main components**
>
> Schematic diagram of a centrifugal pump showing a reservoir at the bottom with a strainer and foot valve, a vertical suction pipe connected to a vacuum gauge and leading to the impeller inside a casing. An arrow from the impeller points to the casing. A delivery pipe extends upward with a delivery valve and a pressure gauge attached, ending with arrows indicating flow direction.



### Components

- The major components of centrifugal pumps are:

    1. Impeller
    2. Casing
    3. Suction Pipe
    4. Delivery pipe
    5. Delivery Valve
    6. Foot valve
    7. Strainer

**Impeller:** - It is the main rotating part of centrifugal pump that provides the centrifugal acceleration to the fluid. It consists of series of backward curved vanes.

**Casing:** - It is an air tight passage surrounding the impeller. It is designed in such a way that the kinetic energy of the water is converted into pressure energy.

**Suction Pipe:** - The suction pipe is used to introduce fluid from the fluid reservoir to the pump. One end of suction pipe is dipped in the fluid reservoir (e.g., water reservoir) and the other end attached to the inlet of the centrifugal pump.

<!-- page 5 | <u>Working</u> -->

**Delivery Pipe: -** The delivery pipe is used to deliver fluid from the pump to the desired height. One end of delivery pipe attached to the outlet of the centrifugal pump and other end at discharge point.

**Foot valve: -** Foot valve allows water only to flow in upward direction.

**Strainer: -** Strainer prevent the entry of foreign particle/material to the pump.

**Delivery valve: -** The delivery valve is fitted at the delivery pipe and it is used to regulate the flow of the fluid.

### <u>Working</u>

When a certain mass of fluid is rotated by an external source, it is thrown away from the central axis of rotation and centrifugal head is impressed which enables it to rise to a higher level. The delivery valve is closed and pump is primed i.e., suction pipe, casing, and portion of delivery pipe up to the delivery valve are filled with water so that no air pocket is left.

Keeping the delivery valve is closed the impeller is rotated by motor, strong suction is created at the eye. Liquid enters the impeller vane from the eye of the pump., come out to casing. Impeller action develops pressure energy as well as velocity energy. Water is lifted through delivery pipe up to required height. When pump is stopped, delivery valve should be closed to prevent back flow from reservoir.

## <u>HYDRAULIC TURBINES</u>

☑ Hydraulic turbines are machines are machines which convert hydraulic energy of water (hydro-power) in to mechanical energy.

☑ A hydraulic turbine consists of a wheel called runner or rotor fitted with number of blades or buckets.

☑ Hydraulic turbines are used to produce power from the potential energy of water stored in the catchment area of a dam.

### <u>Classification of Hydraulic Turbines</u>

☑ According to the action of water turbines are classified as:
  ⇨ ***Impulse turbine:*** All the available pressure energy of water is converted to kinetic energy by means of nozzles and is used to turn the turbine wheel and shaft.
    ⌖ e.g. Pelton wheel
  ⇨ ***Reaction turbine:*** The available pressure energy of water is progressively converted to kinetic energy by means of set of fixed blades and is used to turn the turbine wheel and shaft.
    ⌖ e.g. Francis turbine, Kaplan turbine, Propeller turbine

☑ According to the head of water required turbines are classified as:
  ⇨ ***High head turbine:*** The working head available is above 250 m.
    ⌖ e.g. Pelton wheel.
  ⇨ ***Medium head turbine:*** The working head available between 60 m and 250 m
    ⌖ e.g. Francis turbine.
  ⇨ ***Low head turbine:*** The working head available is below 60 m.
    ⌖ e.g. Kaplan turbine, Propeller turbine

## GEARS

- Gears are toothed wheel used to transmit motion from one shaft to another shaft or between a shaft and a slide.
- The transmission is achieved by successively engaging teeth having special tooth profile.

### Types of Gears

- According to the teeth form:
  - _**Straight gears (spur gears)**_: Teeth are radial and parallel to the axis of the shaft.
  - _**Helical gears**_: Teeth make certain angle with the axis of the shaft.
    - Double helical gears with teeth meeting at the mid of the width id called _**herring bone gears**_.



> **Figure: Diagram showing a helical gear and a spur gear mounted on shafts**
>
> Two diagrams side by side on shafts with red-lined tooth profiles. Left diagram labeled 'Helical gear' shows angled teeth on a cylindrical gear. Right diagram labeled 'Spur gear' shows straight parallel teeth on a cylindrical gear.



- According to the type of gearing:
  - _**External gears**_: Gears of the two shafts mesh externally with each other.
  - _**Internal gears**_: Gears of the two shafts mesh internally with each other.
  - _**Rack and pinion**_: Gear of a shaft meshes with the gears in a straight line.



> **Figure: Diagrams of external gearing, internal gearing, and rack and pinion**
>

○ According to the relative position of shaft axis:

* ***Parallel shaft gears:*** Used to connect two coplanar parallel shafts.
    * e.g. spur gears, helical gears
* ***Intersecting shaft gears:*** Used to connect two coplanar intersecting shafts.
    * e.g. bevel gears
* ***Skew shaft gears:*** Used to connect skew shafts (Non-intersecting and non-parallel shafts at right angle to each other).
    * e.g. worm gears (worm gears are used for higher velocity ratio)



> **Figure: Bevel gear and Worm gear diagrams**

### **Gear ratio (GR)**

It is the ratio of number of teeth on gear to that on pinion is known as Gear ratio.

$$\text{G.R.} = \frac{\text{T}}{\text{t}}$$

$\text{T}$ = Number of teeth on gear  
$\text{t}$ = Number of teeth on pinion

#### **Note:**

* The smallest gear of two mating gears is known as ***Pinion***.
* A gear wheel of infinite diameter is known as ***rack***.

# CLUTCH

- A clutch is a mechanical device that plays a crucial role in power transmission by enabling the engagement and disengagement of rotational motion between a driving shaft and a driven shaft.
- When the clutch is engaged, power flows from the engine to the wheels through the transmission system, propelling the vehicle forward.
- On the other hand, when the clutch is disengaged, power transmission to the wheels is interrupted, causing the vehicle to come to a stop while the engine continues to run.

## Types of Clutches

1. Friction Clutch
2. Single Plate Clutch
3. Multi-plate Clutch
4. Cone Clutch
5. Centrifugal Clutch

## Use of a clutch

- It enables transmitting the torque from the engine to its drivetrain.
- It forms a linkage between the engine flywheel and gearbox.
- Helps in lessening drive-related vibration and jerking.
- Avoids high heat generation to prevent engine malfunctioning, breakdown, etc.
- In some cases, the clutch disengages when the torque exceeds the safety limit, thereby acting as a safety device.
- It helps in efficient power transfer to the vehicle's wheels and helps you in efficient gear shifting.

## EXTRUSION

- Extrusion is process of pressurizing the heated material in a closed container by a ram and the material flow through die opening.
- The pressure is applied either hydraulically or mechanically.
- Usually, the extrusion operation is performs at hot state.
- Some typical examples of components produced by extrusion processes are tubes, rods, hose, structural shapes, long gear profiles etc.
- The amount of reduction that is possible in extrusion is very large as comparing with rolling operation, i.e., the extrusion ratio is very high.

### Methods of extrusion

- Two common methods adapted for extrusion processes are:
  - Direct or forward extrusion
  - Indirect or reverse or backward extrusion

### Direct extrusion

- In this method, the heated metal billet is placed in to the container and the pressure is applied through ram.
- The metal is extruded through die opening in the forward direction, i.e. the same as that of the ram.
- In forward extrusion, some portion of applied force is utilized to overcome the friction between the heated metal billet and the cylinder walls.

### Indirect extrusion

- In indirect extrusion, the billet remains stationary while the die moves into the billet by the hollow ram, through which the backward extrusion takes place.
- Since, there is no friction force between the billet and the container wall, therefore, less force is required by this method (20 to 30% less than direct extrusion)
- However, this process is not widely used because of the difficulty occurred in providing support for the extruded part.

# METAL JOINING PROCESSES

## Welding

- Welding is the process of joining two or more similar or dissimilar metals by creating a metallurgical bonding between them.
- Welding is the most important process in the manufacturing of boiler, ship, aircraft, vehicle, machine, structure, furniture, etc.

### Advantages of Welding

- Welding is more economical and is much faster process as compared to other joining processes like riveting, bolting, etc.
- Large number of metals and alloys both similar and dissimilar can be joined by welding.

### Disadvantages of Welding

- Welding results in residual stresses and distortion of the work pieces and it may need heat treatment for the stress revival.
- Edges preparation of the welding jobs is required before welding.

### Type of Welding

- Based on the type of energy used welding can be classified as:
  - **Gas welding:** Heat from the flame of burning gas is used for welding.
    - e.g., oxy-acetylene welding
  - **Arc welding:** Heat from the arc between electrodes is used for welding.
    - e.g., manual metal arc welding
  - **Resistance welding:** Heat source is the resistance at the welding interface.
    - e.g., resistance spot welding
  - **Chemical welding:** Heat source is a chemical reaction.
    - e.g., thermit welding
  - **Radiant energy welding:** A beam of radiant energy is used for welding.
    - e.g., electron beam welding
  - **Friction welding:** Heat source is the friction at the welding interface.
    - Friction stir welding

## Arc Welding

☑ In this type of welding an electric arc between two electrodes (an electrode and a work piece) is utilized produce heat for melting the weld base metals.

☑ The heat by generated by the electric arc produces a small molten metal pool where filler metal is deposited to get a reinforced welding.

☑ Both direct current (DC) and alternating current (AC) are used for electric arc welding.

☑ The approximate heat distribution between the electrodes is as follows:

⇨ Cathode (negative electrode): 30 % of the total heat generated by the arc.

⇨ Anode (positive electrode): 70 % of the total heat generated by the arc.

## SOLDERING

- Soldering is a method of joining similar or dissimilar metals by heating them to a suitable temperature and joining by means of a filler material called solder.
- The melting temperature of the solder does not exceed $450^{\circ}\text{C}$ and it will be less than that of melting point of base metal.
- Soldering is normally used for neat joints in electronic circuits.

### Applications of Soldering

Some of the applications of Soldering processes are listed below.

- Used for joining in electronic equipment.
- Used for joining in ornaments.
- Used for joining in pipe fittings.
- Used for joining in heat exchangers.

## BRAZING

- Brazing is the joining process in which a filler material is placed between the surfaces to be joined and the temperature of the region is raised enough to melt the filler metal but not work piece.
- The filler material used for brazing has melting temperature above $450^{\circ}\text{C}$ and below that of the base metal to be joined.
- The melted filler material fills the joint and, on cooling, creates a brazed joint.
- Fluxes are applied to the joining surfaces to remove oxides from the surfaces. Borax is the most widely used flux during the process of brazing.

### Applications of Brazing

Some of the applications of Brazing processes are listed below.

- Used for joining in automobile radiators.
- Used for joining in containers and tanks.
- Used for joining in pipe fittings.
- Used for joining in heat exchangers.
- Used for joining in valves.

## LATHE MACHINE

- Lathe machine is one of the oldest machine tools in the metal working industry.
- Lathe machines operates on the principle of a rotating work piece and a fixed cutting tool to shape it into a desired form.
- A lathe machine is a machine tool that is used to remove unwanted material from a rotating work piece in the form of chips.
- The lathe is a machine tool which holds the work piece between two rigid and strong supports called centers or in a chuck or face plate which revolves.

### Lathe Machine Parts

1. **Bed:** The bed is a heavy, rugged casting in which are mounted the working parts of the lathe. It carries the headstock and tail stock and carriage assembly.
2. **Headstock:** The headstock is clamped on the left hand side of the bed. The headstock spindle is a hollow cylindrical shaft that provides a drive from the motor to work holding devices. Gear-box is placed below the headstock and contains a number of different sized gears.
3. **Carriage:** The carriage is located between the headstock and tailstock and serves the purpose of supporting, guiding and feeding the tool against the job during operation. Components of Carriage includes: Cross slide, Compound rest and Tool post
4. **Tailstock:** The tailstock is a movable casting located opposite the headstock on the ways of the bed.

  * Reaming
  * Boring
  * Counter boring
  * Countersinking
  * Tapping

5. **Countersink:** Special angled cone shaped enlargement at the end of the hole to accommodate the screws.
6. **Tapping:** Tapping is the process by which internal threads are formed. It is performed either by hand or by machine.

## MILLING MACHINE

- Milling is a process of removing metal by feeding the work against a rotating multipoint cutter. The machine tool intended for this purpose is known as milling machine.
- Milling is the cutting operation that removes metal by feeding the work against a rotating, cutter having single or multiple cutting edges.
- Flat or curved surfaces of many shapes can be machined by milling with good finish and accuracy.

### Horizontal milling machine



> **Figure: Diagram of a horizontal milling machine showing its various parts.**
>
> Line diagram of a horizontal milling machine. Labelled components include Overhanging arm, Spindle, Arbor, Arbor support, Column, Table, Saddle, Knee, Base, and Elevating screw.



Different parts of a milling machine are:

- **Base**: It is made of cast iron and supports all the other parts of the machine tool.
- **Column**: It is mounted upon the base. It houses the mechanism for providing drive for the spindle.
- **Knee**: It slides up and down on the guide ways of the column. An elevating screw mounted on the base obtains this movement.
- **Saddle**: Saddle is mounted upon the knee and moves in a cross direction. It is mounted on the guide ways of the knee and moves towards or away from the face of the column.
- **Table**: The table is moved longitudinally either by power or manually on the guide ways of the saddle.
- **Spindle**: It is located in the upper part of the column. It receives power from the motor through belt, gears and clutches.

## Operations performed on Milling Machine

- Based on the orientation of the milling cutter axis the milling operations are classified into two basic types:
    - Peripheral milling (Slab milling)
    - End milling (Face milling)

## Peripheral milling (Slab milling)

- In peripheral milling the axis of the cutter is parallel to the work piece surface.
- The milling cutter is provided with number of teeth along its curved periphery.
- The cutter for slab milling may have straight or helical teeth results in, orthogonal and oblique cutting action respectively.
- Peripheral milling is usually performed on a horizontal milling machine.
- Peripheral milling process is further classified into two types:
    - Up milling (Conventional milling)
    - Down milling (Climb milling)

## End milling (Face milling)

- In face milling the cutter is mounted on a spindle having its axis of rotation is perpendicular to the work piece surface.
- The milling cutter is provided with number of teeth on its end face.
- The face milling operation is done on both horizontal and vertical type milling machines.

## Computer numerically control (CNC) Machine

* CNC (Computer Numerical Control) machines are automated tools controlled by computers to execute precise machining operations.
* These cutting-edge devices have redefined the way modern industries approach production processes, offering unparalleled precision, efficiency, and versatility.
* Utilising computer programming and precise instructions, CNC machines can easily shape raw materials into complex shapes with high accuracy.

### Working Principle of CNC Machine



> **Figure: Block diagram of working principle of CNC Machine**
>
> Block diagram showing the architecture of a CNC machine working principle. At the top, 'Input Devices' connects via an arrow to 'Part Programs', which points to 'MCU' (Machine Control Unit). Inside MCU, 'Data Processing Unit' and 'Control Loop Unit' are shown. MCU connects to 'Display Unit' on the right, and outputs 'Motion Data' and 'Miscellaneous Function' downwards to 'Driving System' and 'Machine Tools'. A 'Feedback System' receives position feedback and velocity feedback from the machine tools and loop unit.



* Initially, the part program is input into the MCU (Machine Control Unit) of the CNC system. All data processing occurs within the MCU, and based on the prepared program, it formulates precise motion commands, subsequently transmitting them to the drive system.
* The drive system acts upon these motion commands from the MCU, taking charge of controlling the movement and velocity of the machine tool.

### Components of a CNC Machine

The main parts of the CNC machine are:

**(i) Input Devices:** These devices are responsible for inputting the part program into the CNC machine.

**(ii) Machine Control Unit (MCU):** Serving as the heart of the CNC machine, the MCU performs all controlling actions.

**(iii) Machine Tool:** The CNC machine tool comprises a slide table and a spindle to control the position and speed.

**(iv) Feedback System:** This system incorporates transducers that act as sensors and measuring devices.

**(vi) Display Unit:** A monitor is used as a display unit to showcase CNC machine programs, commands, and other pertinent data
