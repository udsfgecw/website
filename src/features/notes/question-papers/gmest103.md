## Module 1 · Engineering graphics fundamentals

### 2024 · Q1 · 15 marks

> **Question.** The top and front views of line AB are inclined at 35° and 45° to XY. End A lies on both HP and VP, and end B is 50 mm above HP. Draw the projections, find the true length and true inclinations, and locate the traces. The line is in the first quadrant.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** A lies on both planes, so a and a′ fall on XY. Draw the front view a′b′ at 45° to XY from a′, and the top view ab at 35° to XY from a. Make b′ 50 mm above XY and project it down to meet the top-view line; this fixes b. Find the true length by the rotating-line method, and read the true inclinations from the true-length view. The HP trace is where the front view meets XY, and the VP trace is where the top view meets XY.

### 2024 · Q2 · 15 marks

> **Question.** Line PQ is 80 mm long. End P is 50 mm in front of VP and 15 mm above HP. Its top view measures 65 mm, and end Q is 20 mm in front of VP and above HP. Draw the projections, the front view, the true inclinations, and the traces.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Locate p′ 15 mm above XY and p 50 mm in front of XY, and q 20 mm in front of XY. Draw the top view pq = 65 mm. The height difference is $\sqrt{80^2-65^2}=46.6$ mm, so q′ lies 61.6 mm above HP (15 + 46.6). Join p′q′ for the front view. Find the true inclinations from the true-length view, then locate the HP and VP traces where the views meet XY.

### 2025 · Q1 · 15 marks

> **Question.** Line MN is 75 mm long. Its top view measures 65 mm and its front view 55 mm. End M is 20 mm above HP and 10 mm in front of VP. Draw the orthographic projections, find the true inclinations and apparent inclinations, and locate the traces. The line is in the first quadrant.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Locate m′ 20 mm above XY and m 10 mm in front of XY. Draw the top view mn = 65 mm and the front view m′n′ = 55 mm. The height difference of N from M is $\sqrt{75^2-65^2}=37.4$ mm, so n′ lies 57.4 mm above HP. Join m′n′ and mn. The true inclinations come from the true-length view, and the apparent inclinations are the angles the views make with XY. Locate the traces where each view meets XY.

### 2025 · Q2 · 15 marks

> **Question.** End C of line CD is 10 mm above HP and 20 mm in front of VP. The front view is 55 mm long and inclined at 40° to HP, and the top view is 55 mm long. Find the true length and true inclinations, and locate the traces. The line is in the first quadrant.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** In the front view, the horizontal component is $55\cos40^\circ=42.1$ mm and the vertical component is $55\sin40^\circ=35.4$ mm. So d′ is 35.4 mm above c′, that is 45.4 mm above HP. The true length is $\sqrt{42.1^2+35.4^2+35.4^2}\approx65.4$ mm. The inclination to HP is $\sin^{-1}(35.4/65.4)\approx32.8^\circ$. Draw the true-length view to read the inclination to VP, and locate the traces where the views meet XY.

## Module 2 · Orthographic projection

### 2024 · Q3 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A pentagonal prism of 25 mm base edge and 65 mm axis rests with one base edge on HP. Its axis is inclined at 30° to HP and 45° to VP. Draw the projections of the prism.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Use the two-stage method. In stage 1, place the prism with its base on HP and the axis inclined at 30° to HP, with one base edge parallel to VP; draw the front and top views. In stage 2, rotate the top view about the vertical so the axis makes 45° with VP, and project the front view again. Each corner is projected between the two views, and the visible edges are drawn solid.

### 2024 · Q4 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A cone of 40 mm base diameter and 60 mm axis rests on VP on one of its generators. Its front view of the axis is inclined at 35° to HP. Draw its projections.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** In stage 1, place the cone on VP on a generator, with the axis parallel to HP and VP. Draw the front view with the axis at its true length of 60 mm and the base circle in the top view. In stage 2, tilt the front view so the axis makes 35° with HP while the generator stays on VP. Project the base circle and apex into both views, and mark the visible outline.

### 2025 · Q3 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A pentagonal prism of 30 mm base edge and 80 mm axis rests with a base corner on HP. Its slant edge is inclined at 45° to HP and 45° to VP. Draw the projections of the solid.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Use the two-stage method. In stage 1, place the prism with its axis perpendicular to HP and a base corner on HP, and draw the top view as a pentagon with the slant edge in true length. In stage 2, tilt the solid so the slant edge makes 45° with HP and then turn the top view so it makes 45° with VP. Project both views from the final top view, and draw the visible edges solid.

### 2025 · Q4 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A pentagonal pyramid has 30 mm base edges and 50 mm height. It rests on HP on one of its base edges, with its axis at 45° to HP and 40° to VP. Draw the projections of the pyramid.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** In stage 1, place the pyramid on HP with one base edge on HP and the axis parallel to VP; draw the top view showing the base edge in true length and the apex in the front view. In stage 2, rotate the top view so the axis makes 40° with VP, and tilt the front view so the axis makes 45° with HP. Project each corner between the views and show the visible edges solid.

## Module 3 · Sections and developments

### 2024 · Q5 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A right regular hexagonal pyramid of 30 mm base edge and 80 mm axis rests on its base, with one base edge parallel to VP. It is cut by a plane passing through the axis centre and the extreme left base corner, perpendicular to VP. Draw the front view, the sectional top view, and the true shape of the section.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Draw the pyramid with the base on HP. Show the cutting plane as an edge-on line in the front view through the axis and the extreme-left corner. Project where it cuts each edge to the top view, and mark those points on the sectional top view. For the true shape, draw an auxiliary reference parallel to the cutting plane and project each section point perpendicular to it.

### 2024 · Q6 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A cylinder of 50 cm diameter is cut by a 30° auxiliary section plane passing through the extreme right point of its base. The height of the shortest portion of the cylinder, at the extreme left generator, is 1 m. Draw the development of the cut cylinder.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Divide the base circle into equal parts and draw the generators. Mark the section points on each generator where the 30° plane cuts it. For development, draw a stretch-out of length $\pi D=\pi(500)=1570.8$ mm, divide it into the same number of equal parts, and erect generators of their true lengths. Transfer each section height from the front view to its matching generator and join the points smoothly.

### 2025 · Q5 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A cylinder of 60 mm height and 50 mm diameter rests on HP on its base. A section plane perpendicular to VP and inclined at 40° to HP cuts the axis 10 mm below the top face. Draw the projections of the sectioned cylinder and the true shape of the section.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Draw the cylinder with its axis perpendicular to HP. In the front view, mark the axis point 10 mm below the top and draw the plane through it at 40° to XY. Divide the base circle into equal parts, project the generators to the front view, and mark where they meet the plane. Project these points back to the top view and hatch the cut. The true shape is an ellipse; draw it using an auxiliary reference parallel to the cutting plane.

### 2025 · Q6 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A right circular cone of 50 mm base diameter and 60 mm height rests on HP on its base. A section plane perpendicular to VP and inclined at 30° to HP bisects the axis. Draw the development of the truncated cone.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** The slant height is $L=\sqrt{25^2+60^2}=65$ mm. The plane bisects the axis, so it cuts at 30 mm above the base. Find the apex-to-section distance on each generator from the front view. For a radial-line development, draw an arc of radius 65 mm with included angle $360^\circ\times(25/65)\approx138.5^\circ$, divide it into the same number of generator intervals as the base, and transfer the true lengths along each generator. Join the transferred points to get the truncated edge.

## Module 4 · Isometric views and projections

### 2024 · Q7 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A hemisphere of 50 mm radius rests centrally on the top of a square prism of 40 mm base edge and 60 mm height, with its circular face upward. Draw the isometric projection of the solids.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Draw the square prism using isometric scale for its 40 mm edges and 60 mm height. Locate the centre of its top face. The hemisphere's curved surface touches the prism top at the centre point, and its 100 mm flat face overhangs the 40 mm square. Draw the circular face using the isometric ellipse construction, then complete the dome from it. Confirm in the original figure that the hemisphere sits on the prism as stated.

### 2024 · Q8 · 15 marks

> **Question.** Draw the isometric view of a pentagonal pyramid of 30 mm base side and 60 mm height, resting with its base centrally on top of a cylinder of 90 mm diameter and 50 mm height. One base edge of the pyramid is perpendicular to VP.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Draw the cylinder first using isometric ellipses for its 90 mm circular ends and its 50 mm height. On the top ellipse, locate the centre and inscribe a regular pentagon of 30 mm side, with the specified edge perpendicular to VP. Project the five corners, place the apex 60 mm above the base centre, and join the apex to each corner. Show the visible edges solid.

### 2025 · Q7 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A sphere of 70 mm diameter is kept centrally on top of a cylindrical slab of 60 mm diameter and 10 mm thickness. Draw the isometric view of the combination.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Draw the slab with isometric ellipses for its 60 mm top and bottom faces, 10 mm apart. Mark the centre of the top face. The sphere has a 35 mm radius, so it overhangs the slab by 5 mm on each side. Draw the sphere centred on the vertical through the slab centre, and show its outline and the contact with the slab top.

### 2025 · Q8 · 15 marks

> **Question.** A sphere of 25 mm radius is kept on top of a rectangular block of 80 mm length, 60 mm width and 15 mm thickness. It rests at a point 30 mm from each of two adjacent top edges. Draw the isometric projection of the combination, with the longer block edges perpendicular to XY in the top view and the sphere seen away from the observer.
>
> ---
>
> **Solution.** Draw the block using the isometric scale (0.816) for its 80, 60 and 15 mm dimensions. Locate the contact point 30 mm from each of the two chosen top edges. Raise the sphere centre 25 mm above that point, and draw the sphere with its isometric projection diameter of $2(25)(0.816)\approx40.8$ mm, placed behind the block so it is seen away from the observer.
