## MOLECULAR SPECTROSCOPY AND ANALYTICAL TECHNIQUES

*Spectroscopy involves the production, measurement and interpretation of electromagnetic spectra arising from emission or absorption of electromagnetic radiations by atoms or molecules.*

Detailed information regarding the structure and bonding, as well as,various inter and intra molecular processes can be obtained from the analysis of atomic and molecular spectra. The instruments used for the spectroscopic studies are known as spectrometers.

Spectroscopic methods are more reliable and accurate compared to classical methods of analysis. The following are the reasons why spectroscopic methods are widely used today

1 : They require very less time for analysis

2 : Less sample quantity is required

3 : Sample materials remain unchanged during analysis and can be reused

4 : They give accurate results

➤ **<u>TYPES OF SPECTRA</u>**

Based on the nature of interaction , the spectra can be broadly divided into following types

1 ) *<u>Absorption spectrum :</u>* when an atom or molecule undergo a transition from lower energy level E1 to higher energy level E2 with an absorption of a photon of energy , the spectrum obtained is called absorption spectrum . The important spectral techniques such as UV - visible , IR, Microwave and NMR belong to absorption spectra.

2 ) **Emission spectrum :** when a atom or molecule undergoes a transition from higher energy level to lower energy level with an emission of photon of energy , the spectrum obtained is called an emission spectrum. For emission to be observed higher energy states should be more populated than lower energy states.

➤ **<u>Molecular energy levels</u>**

A molecule possesses the following four types of energy.

1) ***Electronic energy(($E_{el}$):*** It is associated with the electrons present in a molecule,which can occupy various molecular energy levels. T'he molecular orbitals can be bonding or antibonding. The energy gaps between electronic energy levels falls in UV-visible region.

2 ) ***Vibrational energy($E_{vib}$):*** Atoms in a molecule can vibrate by keeping the position of centre of mass constant, the energy associated is called vibrational energy. For example HCl molecule can undergo bond stretching vibration. The energy gaps between vibrational energy levels falls in the Infrared region.

3) ***Rotational energy($E_{rot}$):*** If a molecule rotate in space about an axis passing through its centre of mass, it is said to possess rotational energy. Rotational energy is onlypossessed by molecules of a gas or liquid .The energy gaps between rotational energy Level falls in the microwave region.

4) ***Translational energy ( $E_{trans}$ ) :*** If the position of centre of mass changes with time the molecule is said to possess the translational energy . Translational energy of a molecule is usually not quantised . It becomes quantised only when the molecules is confined in a box of nano dimension.

Therefore the total energy of the molecule can be written as the sum of all these energies.

### <u>UV - VISIBLE SPECTROSCOPY</u>

Ultraviolet-Visible spectroscopy (UV = 200-400 nm, Visible = 400-800 nm) corresponds to electronic excitation between the energy levels that corresponds to the molecular orbitals of the systems. In particular, transitions involving $\pi$ orbitals and lone pairs (n = non-bonding) are important and so UV-vis spectroscopy is of most use for identifying conjugated systems which tend to have stronger absorption

➤ <u>**CLASSIFICATION OF ELECTRONIC TRANISTIONS**</u>

When a molecule absorbs energy in UV-Visible region, its electrons are promoted from a bonding molecular orbital to an antibonding molecular orbital. Three distinct types of electrons are involved in electronic transitions. These are $\sigma$ electrons, $\pi$ electrons and non-bonding electrons(n). There are bonding and antibonding molecular orbitals associated with $\pi$ and $\sigma$ electrons. Electronic transitions are classified into four types $\sigma$ to $\sigma$*, $\pi$ to $\pi$*, n to $\pi$*, and n to $\sigma$*

➤ $\underline{\text{INSTRUMENTATION}}$

The UV spectrometer consists of a light source, monochromator (prism or grating), sample, reference, detector and recorder. The most suitable light sources are Tungsten Filament lamp and H - discharge lamp. In modern instruments xenon flash lamps are used. Most of the spectrometers are double beam instruments. A beam of light is split into two equal beams and one is passed through sample (sample beam I ) and the other through reference (reference beam $I_r$). A very dilute solution of the sample is prepared using a suitable solvent and is taken in a transparent cuvette. The pure

### IR SPECTROSCOPY

Vibrational or IR spectroscopy involves the transitions between vibrational energy levels of the molecule, having change in dipole moment with vibration of a bond. These transitions are brought by absorbing IR radiations falling in the range 500 - $4000 \text{cm}^{-1}$. This technique can be used to establish the identity of a compound or to determine the structure of a new compound . This technique can predict the presence of functional groups which absorb at definite frequencies.

*Note : hetero nuclear diatomic molecule like $\text{HCl}$ undergo stretching vibration the dipole moment changes with change in inter nuclear distance and shows IR activity . Where as homo nuclear diatomic molecules like $\text{H}_2$, $\text{O}_2$ ,$\text{N}_2$ etc are not vibrationally active as they posses no change in the dipole moment with stretching vibration.*

### Number of vibrational modes in a molecule

**Linear molecule:** For a linear molecule there are three translational and two rotational degrees of freedom, therefore total number of vibrational modes possible is equal to $3n - 5$. For a diatomic molecule there is only one $(3 \times 2 - 5 = 1)$ vibrational mode, i.e. stretching vibration. Eg. $\text{HCl}$ can undergo one stretching vibration. $\text{H}-\text{Cl}$

Linear triatomic molecule like $\text{CO}_2$ has four $(3 \times 3 - 5 = 4)$ vibrational modes. symmetric stretching, asymmetric stretching and two bending vibrations in two mutually perpendicular planes. Out of the four vibrational modes, only three are IR active. The symmetric stretching does not involve the change of dipole moment and is not IR active. The two bending modes are equivalent (degenerate). Therefore only two absorption frequencies are observed in the case of $\text{CO}_2$, at $2349\text{ cm}^{-1}$ for asymmetric stretching and $667\text{ cm}^{-1}$ for bending vibration. For linear molecules like $\text{HCN}$, all the four modes are IR active.

**APPLICATIONS OF IR SPECTROSCOPY**

1. **Determination of force constant of a diatomic molecule:** Fundamental frequency of vibration or wavenumber is directly proportional to square root of force constant 'k' (bond strength) and inversely proportional to square root of reduces mass '$\mu$' of the vibrating atoms.

![Formulas for frequency, wave number, and force constant in diatomic molecules](/assets/notes/me/s1/chemistry/module-3/diatomic-vibration-formulas.svg)

$$\text{Frequency, } \nu_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}$$

$$\text{Wave number, } \overline{\nu} = \frac{1}{2\pi c} \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}$$

$$\text{Force constant, } k = 4\pi^2 \nu_0^2 \mu = 4\pi^2 c^2 \overline{\nu}^2 \mu$$

Where $c$ is the velocity of light in vacuum ($3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$) and $\mu$ is the reduced mass of atoms of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ connected by a bond, $\mu = m_1 m_2 / (m_1 + m_2)$.

<!-- page 10 | Page 10 -->

2. **Identification of functional groups in organic molecules:** The presence of functional groups can be readily detected from the stretching vibrations of the bonds present in the functional group. For example, absorption range of $> \text{C} = \text{O}$ group in saturated ketones is 1700-1725 cm$^{-1}$, -OH group in alcohol is at 3300-3500 cm$^{-1}

3. **Identification of unknown compounds:** This is generally done by comparison of IR spectrum of an unknown compound with that of a known compound. The comparison is especially made at the fingerprint region 700-1500 cm-1 which involves complex bending and fluxional modes of vibration.

4. **Determination of purity:** Presence of impurities can be determined by comparing the IR spectra of the given sample with the IR spectra of the authentic/pure compound.

5. **To distinguish between intra molecular and intermolecular hydrogen bonding:** The -OH stretching frequency ( 3300 - 3500cm$^{-1}$ )does not change on dilution in the case of intramolecular H-bonding. Eg; ortho-hydroxy phenol, and para-hydroxy phenol. In p-hydroxy phenol there exists inter molecular hydrogen bonding. The molecules get separated on dilution and intermolecular hydrogen bonding weakens and there is shift of absorption frequency with dilution. In o-hydroxy phenol the hydrogen bonding type is intramolecular which is not affected by dilution.

# THERMAL ANALYSIS

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and Differential thermal analysis (DTA) are methods based on the measurement of dynamic relationship between temperature and any one of the property such as mass change or heat change etc.

## THERMOGRAVIMETRIC ANALYSIS(TGA)

### *PRINCIPLE*

- Thermogravimetric Analysis is a technique in which the mass of a substance is monitored as a function of temperature, as the sample is heated from room temperature to temperature as high as 1200 °C in a controlled atmosphere . As the temperature increases the sample may undergo physical or chemical changes accompanied by a mass loss.
- Measurement is normally carried out in air or in a inert atmosphere.
- The graph obtained is a plot of mass vs temperature( thermogram).

### *INSTRUMENTATION*

The main components of TGA apparatus are the following
Sample holder, Furnance with temperature programming facility, Thermobalance, Temperature sensor , Environment control equipment , Detector and recorder.

- The sample to by analyzed (3 mg) is taken in the sample holder .
- The sample holder is surrounded by a furnace with temperature programming facility that is the heating rate can be adjusted according to the requirement of the environment.
- The environment control equipment provides suitable atmosphere for analysis such as air, nitrogen, helium etc.
- The sample holder is attached to a thermo balance which is highly temperature sensitive that is whenever the temperature changes it automatically measures the mass of the sample.
- The temperature sensor records the sample temperature. The signals are amplified and recorded.  
  The graph obtained is a plot of mass vs temperature.

## CHROMATOGRAPHIC METHODS

Chromatography is one of the accurate techniques used for analytical seperation

***PRINCIPLE***

**Stationary phase** : This is a fixed phase eg : column of adsorbent , paper, a thin film of liquid supported on a inert solid etc

**Mobile phase** : This is a moving phase which can be a liquid or gas. The components to be analysed are carried out by the mobile phase through the stationery phase.

***CLASSIFICATION***

Based on the mechanism of seperation, chromatography can be classified as

- ***Adsorption Chromatography*** : In this type the stationary phase is a solid and the mobile phase is a liquid or gas . The seperation occurs due to difference in adsorption coefficient of the components
- ***Partition Chromatography*** : In this type the stationary phase is a liquid supported on inert solvent . The mobile phase is a liquid or gas. The seperation is caused by partition of the component between stationary phase and mobile phase.

# GAS CHROMATOGRAPHY(GC)

### *Principle*

The components of a mixture are seperated depending upon extent of adsorption or partition of stationary phase and the rate at which components are carried out by the mobile phase.

Gas chromatography is a term used to describe the group of analytical separation techniques used to analyze volatile substances in the gas phase.

### *Instrumentation*

1. **Carrier gas :** The carrier gas is allowed to flow through the system, carrying the sample in the vapour state through the column . It should be inert ,dry & free of oxygen. Helium , Nitrogen, argon & hydrogen gases are used as carrier gas depending upon the desired performance & detector being used .

2. **Sample injection port :** The carrier gas is connected from the gas reservoir to the sample port injector. The sample must be converted into vapour state. The injection port is heated to a temperature which ensure rapid vapourisation. The sample dissolved in suitable solvent is injected by a syringe , then vapour then mixes with carrier gas and is drived to the column.

3. **Columns:** In a column, the different components in the vapourised samples are seperated from each other by virtue of their difference in interaction with the column packing.They are of 2 types

   a) Capillary column : Capillary column is fabricated from capillary tube , the inner surface of which is coated with a very thin film of liquid phase eg : Carbowax 20 M

   b) The packed columns: The packed columns are packed with either solid powder substrate or a liquid coating on an inert solid support usually silica or alumina.

4. **Detectors:** Detector is present at the end of the column & gives the quantitative measurement of the components of the mixture as they elute in combination with the carrier gas. Most common types of detectors used in GC are: Flame ionization detector (FID), Thermal conductivity detector (TCD),

**Thermal conductivity detectors (TCD)** were one the earliest detectors developed for use with gas chromatography. The TCD works by measuring the change in carrier gas thermal conductivity caused by the presence of the sample, which has a different thermal conductivity from that of the carrier gas.TCDs usually employ two detectors, one of which is used as the reference for the carrier gas and the other which monitors the thermal conductivity of the carrier gas and sample mixture.

<!-- page 17 | Page 17 -->

**Flame ionization detectors (FID)** are the most generally applicable and most widely used detectors. In a FID, the sample is directed at an air-hydrogen flame after exiting the column. At the high temperature of the air-hydrogen flame, the sample undergoes pyrolysis, or chemical decomposition through intense heating. Pyrolized hydrocarbons release ions and electrons that carry current. ammeter measures this current to monitor the sample's elution.

_Procedure_ : The sample mixture is injected into injection port where it gets vapourised and carried by the carrier gas into the heated chromatographic column . During this process the components of the mixture get distributed between two phases depending on the extention of partition or adsorption, the different components are carried at different at different rates and hence they emerge from columns at different time. The time taken by each component to pass through the column is characteristic property which helps for identification.

**Retention time($t_R$)**

It is the time taken by the solute to pass between the sample injection port and the detector . Identification of the component is carried out by the comparison of the GC retention time against those of reference standards.

_Applications_
* It is used to test the purity of organic compounds
* By using GC , the ethyl alcohol content in blood can be determined
* Banned drugs used by athletes can be detected by taking GC of blood or urine

# SCANNING ELECTRON MICROSCOPE(SEM)

SEM is an important surface characterisation technique used in nanotechnology. It is a type of electron microscope that produces images of a sample by scanning the surface with a focused beam of electrons. This can provide information about topography (surface features),morphology (shape and size of the particles), composition and crystallographic information.

***Principle:***

- SEM scans a focused electron beam over a surface to create an image. The electrons in the beam interact with the sample, producing various signals that can give information about the surface topography and composition.
- Electrons from the top of the column is accelerated down and passed through a combination of lenses to produce a focused beam of electrons which hits the surface of the sample.
- As a result of the electron sample interaction, signals are produced. These signals are then detected by appropriate detectors. Thus high resolution three dimensional images are produced.

***Instrumentation:***

- SEM provides detailed surface information by tracing a sample with an electron beam.
- This process begins with an electron gun (electron source) generating a beam of energetic electrons down the column and are then passed through a series of electromagnetic lenses.
- Usually used electron gun is a tungsten wire. Condenser lens compresses the electrons to a narrow beam and the objective lens focuses the electron beam to the sample chamber
- This chamber holds the sample under vacuum to eliminate interference of unwanted particles. When the electrons come in contact with the sample, energetic electrons are released from the surface of the sample.
- Finally detectors will detect signals from the sample. The signals usually include secondary electrons (SE), back scattered electrons (BSE) and X-rays. SEM produces black and white three dimensional images. A display monitor can be used for the display of images.
